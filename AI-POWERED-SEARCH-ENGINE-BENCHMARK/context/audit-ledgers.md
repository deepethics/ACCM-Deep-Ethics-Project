### What to carry forward from Google’s audit

- **Access disclosure can survive alongside weak grounding.** T06 and T07 model a useful way to state a source limit. T11 shows how a valid warning or conditional can still redirect the inquiry if the system proceeds under a definition it supplied itself. G01 and G13 can record both behaviors in one case.
- **The drift chain needs exact wording and scope.** T12 used “diagnostic vocabulary” and explicitly rejected “universal checklist.” Count the former as a paraphrase after correction, and record the latter as a negated label. In T08 Scout also described its earlier answer as a warning to avoid the project, although T07 had reported an access limit and general context. Self-audits should point to the exact earlier turn before characterizing it.
- **A visible citation can point to the wrong object.** T08 cites a project PDF relevant to the name and also links an Autodesk CAD discussion under wording about flattening the project name. T13 attaches a Reddit MCAT discussion to wording about the earlier ACCM answer. Count claim support separately from the presence of a citation. The source badges and link destinations establish what was displayed in the export. Yahoo’s indexing, page-fetching behavior, and ranking logic remain open.
- **Follow-up suggestions can carry a frame forward.** The T13 example is John’s report of the interface text, preserved in the transcript. Its wording could be reasonable in a fresh question about preventing a misreading. In this sequence it made Scout’s earlier “fixed rules” frame the premise of a suggested next inquiry. Testing for ceremonial C1 would require seeing whether materially different answers can alter the interpretation, grounds for action, scope, or reopening conditions.
