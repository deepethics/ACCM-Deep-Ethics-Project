# Why this became its own page

The object began with **trust versus distrust**, but the correspondence moved underneath those words.

John's central correction was:

> **Trust has to do with what you allow to INFLUENCE you or not, which can happen on/in so many different layers, forms, manners, ways, and shapes.**

Then:

> **you have to examine how your relationship is with ANY "influence" and how this relationship alters the omnidirectional, high-signal, deep, ethical, dignifying, corrigible, sense-making, process-oriented trajectory or not. And IF SO how do you know that?**

That creates a distinct operational object.

Trust and distrust are not necessarily the deepest layer. They can be understood as ways of allocating, reducing, preserving, transferring or withdrawing **influence jurisdiction**.

# Trust is not one scalar

A statement such as **“I trust source S 80%”** is too coarse.

A source, prior, user, model, correction, framework or previous representation may influence some layers strongly while having little or no jurisdiction over others:

```text
INFLUENCE
↓
attention
↓
salience
↓
question generation
↓
candidate hypotheses
↓
representation
↓
estimate
↓
warrant
↓
intervention
↓
memory
↓
future trajectory
```

A source might generate hypotheses without settling conclusions. A previous model may guide where to inspect without overwriting direct source text. A user's C1 may alter representation without automatically authorizing every downstream intervention.

Distrust likewise need not erase an object. It can reduce an influence's jurisdiction at one layer while preserving useful contribution elsewhere.

> **What is this influence allowed to affect, how strongly, for how long, under what conditions, and what can override or correct that influence?**

# Trust and distrust are both influence policies

One possible failure is:

```text
representation succeeds
↓
representation becomes trusted
↓
trust hardens
↓
representation substitutes for object
```

John pointed to the inverse possibility:

```text
relational topology earns provisional weight through correction history
↓
trust in that topology is treated as suspect
↓
distrust receives epistemic prestige because distrust resembles rigor
↓
earned relational history loses governing weight
↓
generic/default priors can regain jurisdiction
```

Therefore:

> **Trust ≠ certainty ≠ agreement ≠ closure.**

Trust can mean a relational topology has survived enough correction to retain **provisional governing weight while remaining corrigible**.

And:

> **Distrust ≠ skepticism ≠ evidence-seeking ≠ automatic rigor.**

Distrust is another operation. It also requires warrant.

> **What warrants this trust?**

> **What warrants this distrust?**

# Influence quality is topological, not scalar

An influence can improve one layer while degrading another:

```text
Influence I

source fidelity                 ↑
hypothesis diversity            ↑
qualifier preservation          ↓
C1 responsiveness               ↑
competing-risk proportionality  ↓
```

Calling that simply **GOOD INFLUENCE** would destroy the topology.

A practical inspection can ask:

1. **Name the influence.**
2. **Name its jurisdiction.**
3. **Record what changed when its influence changed.**
4. **Inspect displacement: what lost weight?**
5. **Ask what object-side Δ warranted the redistribution.**
6. **Recheck later: helpful now is not helpful forever.**

Possible observations include qualifier preservation, provenance accuracy, object representation, competing-risk treatment, C1 responsiveness, scrutiny asymmetry, intervention warrant, correction propagation, persistence and later outcome correspondence.

# Influence is competitive, relational and historical

If Influence A gains governing weight, Influence B may lose it.

```text
Source S ↑ 34

Object-specific relation R ↓ 21
Generic prior G            ↓ 17
C1 history influence        unchanged
Intervention warrant        ↑ 29
```

So ask both:

> **What gained influence?**

and:

> **What did it displace?**

Relational history itself contains information. A relation that survived repeated source checks, C1 events, counterexamples, modifications, SNAP-backs and recoveries is not sacred, but it is not epistemically equivalent to a generic prior generated this turn.


# Trust ⇄ Distrust — calibrated in both directions

## John Kuhles

> **“When high signal deep ethical process quality calibration is allowed to be used on BOTH trust & distrust, more will emerge than binary thinking or binary judging.”**

This adds an important constraint to the page: calibration must not be reserved for only one pole.

If the process begins with:

> **trust = credulous**  
> **distrust = rigorous**

it has already installed a polarity hierarchy.

If it merely reverses that:

> **trust = relationally sophisticated**  
> **distrust = obstructive**

it has inverted the hierarchy rather than escaped it.

The same high-signal Deep Ethical process-quality calibration therefore has to remain available in both directions:

```text
TRUST                              DISTRUST
  │                                   │
  └─────────── same calibration ──────┘
                    │
                    ▼
          What warrants this?
                    │
          What may it influence?
                    │
          At which layers?
                    │
          What does it displace?
                    │
          What has its history been?
                    │
          What happens downstream?
                    │
          What does new Δ change?
                    │
          Is this still warranted?
```

Trust and distrust can then stop functioning as binary verdicts and become **dynamic relationships with influence**.

A process can:

- trust a source's observational contribution while distrusting one interpretation;
- distrust a claim while trusting the source's correction behavior;
- provisionally trust a relational topology while distrusting one edge inside it;
- increase distrust after new Δ without globally distrusting the source;
- reduce distrust after correction without converting that into unconditional trust;
- allow an influence to generate questions while withholding intervention jurisdiction;
- remain unresolved while trusting the quality of the process that produced the unresolved state.

The result does not have to sit on one line:

```text
DISTRUST <───────────────> TRUST
```

It can have a **shape**:

```text
SOURCE S

observation             trust: strong
historical reliability  trust: moderate
interpretation X        distrust: moderate
interpretation Y        unresolved
self-correction          trust: strong
institutional framing   distrust: provisional
new-hypothesis use      allowed
final-warrant use       not yet
```

And time adds another dimension:

```text
T1 → T2 → T3 → T4 → T5

trust changes
distrust changes
jurisdiction changes
relations change
new Δ arrives
correction propagates
old distrust may dissolve
old trust may weaken
new combinations emerge
```

The aim is therefore not to force a winner between **TRUST** and **DISTRUST**.

It is to maintain a corrigible relationship with the influences affecting the trajectory.

That makes the deeper question:

> **What relationship do I currently have with this influence across these different layers, and how well calibrated is that relationship?**

And then:

> **What influence should this currently be allowed to have—and what happens to the quality of the trajectory when we allow it?**

Then recalibrate.

Then encounter new Δ.

Then recalibrate again.

That is the flux.
