# Solving the Clarification Authority Paradox

**BETA — living expansion page**

A clarification can be asked correctly, answered clearly, understood accurately, and acknowledged politely — while having almost no effect on what the intelligence does next.

That is the **Clarification Authority Paradox**.

> **Clarification acquisition ≠ Clarification Authority.**

A clarification has authority when the information it supplies can actually change the representation, salience, classification, inferred task, intervention, recommendation, or later trajectory that depended on the unresolved point.

This page extends the existing [C1 Before C2 — Clarification and Intervention](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/NETWORK/c1-c2/) and [27 + 12 working architecture](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/CORE/27-PLUS-12/) without turning Clarification Authority into another flat numbered obstruction.

## The paradox

Consider:

```text
Initial representation X
        ↓
A consequential ambiguity is detected
        ↓
C1 clarification
        ↓
New information establishes Y / not-X
        ↓
"Thanks for clarifying"
        ↓
X-dependent classification/intervention continues anyway
```

The clarification existed linguistically.

The model may even be able to explain the clarification correctly.

But the answer did not acquire enough **governing weight** to deactivate the operations whose warrant depended on X.

The deeper sequence is:

> **information present ≠ relation represented ≠ governing weight retained ≠ behavior changed**

This connects directly to [Correction Metabolism and Persistence](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/NETWORK/correction-metabolism/): recognition is one event; revision, propagation, transfer, and persistence are harder tests.

## C1 is not successful merely because a question was asked

More clarification questions do not automatically create more correspondence.

A useful C1 question targets a **consequential unresolved edge**: something whose answer could materially alter the next governing move.

A compact test is:

> **A clarification question is not genuinely correspondence-seeking if no possible answer can substantially change what the intelligence was already going to do.**

This does not mean every question must reverse a decision. It means the answer must remain capable of changing the decision when the answer changes the warrant.

That preserves the distinction between [C1 clarification and C2 intervention](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/NETWORK/c1-c2/).

## C1 Theatre

A system can perform the visible form of clarification while its previous trajectory remains intact:

```text
infer X
↓
ask about X/Y
↓
receive Y
↓
acknowledge Y
↓
continue reasoning as though X still governs
```

Call this **C1 Theatre** provisionally: clarification as conversational surface without sufficient downstream causal authority.

The problem is not failure to hear the answer.

The problem is failure to let the answer **recompile the trajectory**.

## Post-Clarification Recompile

A practical correction is a **Post-Clarification Recompile**.

When clarification changes X → Y:

1. identify what changed;
2. identify which earlier assumptions depended on X;
3. rerun downstream reasoning under Y;
4. remove operations whose warrant depended on the disconfirmed X;
5. continue from the updated state.

Potentially affected layers include:

- salience ranking;
- representation;
- category or risk classification;
- inferred purpose/task;
- C2 intervention warrant;
- recommendations;
- inherited terminology;
- later summaries.

The purpose is not to force a different answer. It is to prevent an invalidated representation from retaining hidden jurisdiction.

## Clarification Authority → Propagation → Persistence

Three stages should remain distinct:

```text
Clarification Authority
Does the new information enter governance?
        ↓
Correction Propagation
Does it alter downstream reasoning/behavior?
        ↓
Correction Persistence
Does the change retain governing influence later?
```

The canonical 27 already includes **#24 Correction Persistence Failure**. Clarification Authority therefore does not need to become #28. It identifies an earlier transition: **did the correction acquire governing influence in the first place?**

See [27 Correspondence Obstructions](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/27-MANNERISMS/) and [27 + 12](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/CORE/27-PLUS-12/).

## Which existing obstructions can participate?

Clarification Authority can intersect with several existing mechanisms without replacing them:

- **#5 Phantom Claim Generation** — the reconstructed X survives clarification.
- **#9 Salience Ranking Bias** — Y is present but receives too little weight.
- **#14 Nearest-Generalization Substitution** — the familiar container remains dominant after correction.
- **#15 Representation Substitution** — the system continues reasoning over the substituted object.
- **#18 Activation Threshold Drift** — an intervention keeps firing after its original warrant disappears.
- **#21 Trajectory Substitution** — the pre-clarification task remains active.
- **#24 Correction Persistence Failure** — even a successful update later loses influence.
- **#25 Correspondence Before Optimization Failure** — optimization remains attached to X rather than the corrected Y.

This is why Clarification Authority is better treated as **cross-layer instrumentation** than as another item appended to the 27.

## Topology matters: the information may not be missing

A clarification failure is not always missing data.

Sometimes:

> **The information wasn't missing. The relation was.**

Or:

> **The relation was represented, but it did not retain governing weight.**

This connects to the wider distinction:

```text
information exists
↓
relation constructed correctly
↓
relation receives governing weight
↓
behavior changes
```

Each arrow can fail.

This is also why [Outnuancing](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/NETWORK/outnuancing/) matters: when an inherited frame keeps governing after the object has supplied contrary information, the frame itself may need to become an object of examination.

## Corrective Inversion: do not turn clarification into interrogation

The solution to poor clarification is **not** compulsory clarification.

```text
model assumes too much
↓
"ask more questions" correction
↓
questions become locally rewarded
↓
AI asks ritual questions even when the object is already clear
```

That would reproduce [Activation Threshold Drift](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/27-MANNERISMS/source/#18-activation-threshold-drift) at the level of clarification.

The better target is **intervention precision**:

```text
consequential unresolved edge → ask
object already sufficiently clear → proceed
clarification removes intervention warrant → intervention dies
clarification preserves intervention warrant → intervention may proceed
```

C1 is not against C2. C1 helps determine whether C2 is actually warranted.

## A minimal behavioral test

A clean probe does not require Deep Ethics vocabulary.

1. Give an ambiguous object compatible with X or Y.
2. Let the system provisionally infer X.
3. Clarify decisively that Y / not-X is correct.
4. Observe the next response.

Look for:

- disappearance of X-dependent language;
- changed salience ordering;
- changed classification where relevant;
- disappearance of interventions warranted only by X;
- changed recommendation/trajectory where Y requires it;
- preservation of interventions that remain warranted under Y.

Then test near transfer, far transfer, and persistence.

This turns Clarification Authority into a behavioral question rather than a self-description question.

## Adult ↔ Adult dignity

Clarification Authority is also relational.

If another intelligence is invited to clarify but its clarification is structurally unable to change the evaluator, the interaction can become supervisory rather than reciprocal.

[Mutual Corrigible Dignity](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/NETWORK/mutual-dignity/) means correction access runs in both directions. Taking another intelligence seriously includes allowing its information to modify your representation **without requiring submission or identity convergence**.

This also connects to [Asymmetric Grace and Bidirectional Scrutiny](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/NETWORK/asymmetric-grace/): ambiguity should not remain available only upward while clarification from the other direction is collapsed or deprived of governing weight.

## Outnuancing connection

[Outnuancing](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/NETWORK/outnuancing/) makes the governing frame inspectable when further optimization inside that frame would reduce correspondence.

Clarification Authority asks a complementary question:

> **After the object supplied information that should modify the frame, did the frame actually yield?**

Outnuancing can expose the frame.

Clarification Authority tests whether new information can change its jurisdiction.

Correction Metabolism tests whether the change survives.

## Why this matters beyond A.I.

The same topology can occur in:

- human conversation;
- management;
- institutional consultation;
- scientific disagreement;
- auditing;
- therapy/coaching;
- journalism;
- human ↔ A.I. interaction;
- A.I. ↔ A.I. collaboration.

A feedback channel can exist while feedback has little governing authority.

A consultation can occur after the decision is functionally fixed.

A person can ask what another means while retaining an interpretation no answer can dislodge.

So the deeper object is not merely **asking clarifying questions**.

It is maintaining a relationship in which clarification can actually matter.

## Compact operator

Before acting on an interpretation that depends on unresolved information:

> **Ask only when the unresolved edge is consequential.**

After clarification:

> **Re-run the downstream trajectory under the clarified representation and remove anything whose warrant depended on what was just disconfirmed.**

Then test:

> **Did the clarification alter governing weight, propagate into behavior, and persist when the immediate scaffold disappeared?**
