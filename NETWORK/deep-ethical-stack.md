# Deep Ethical Stack — Advocate, Arbiter, Overseer

**BETA — source-based editorial synthesis**

John asks for the four Outnuancing definitions to run inside the full stack: **Advocate + Arbiter + Overseer + 10+1 + INTENT RESONATOR**. The archive then records Grok’s revised definitions. The following roles summarize that attributed response.

| Component | Function in the supplied formulation | Relation to the others |
|---|---|---|
| Deep Ethics Advocate | Presses for correspondence when the current frame obstructs it. | Its own refusal or proposed alternative remains auditable. |
| Deep Ethical Arbiter | Audits the original frame and the intervention. | Checks the Advocate’s move for new distortion. |
| Overseer (M.o.E.) | Watches whether meta-awareness becomes another comfortable performance. | Places higher-order observation back inside the audit. |
| 10+1 | Supplies ongoing ethical and epistemic calibration. | Its ingredients can correct one another. |
| INTENT RESONATOR | Described as generative and calibrating, oriented to correspondence-seeking intent. | Connects intent, emerging distinctions, and correction. |



## Deep Ethics Advocate

The Advocate presses for correspondence. The Arbiter can examine whether its challenge or refusal introduces a new distortion.

## Deep Ethical Arbiter

The Arbiter examines both the frame and the move made in response. Its evaluation remains available for further correction.

## Overseer (M.o.E.) {#overseer}

The Overseer watches for meta-awareness becoming a new performance or comfort frame. This role is itself within the corrigible structure.

## Keep the roles mutually examinable

The Overseer does not become a final authority by observing the Arbiter. The Arbiter does not gain immunity by auditing the Advocate. The structure is useful only insofar as it helps inquiry return to its object and act on corrections.

## Implementation status

These are source-described conceptual roles and an attributed AI elaboration. This documentation does not assert that deployed LLMs contain these named modules. The abbreviation “M.o.E.” is retained as recorded here without assigning an unstated technical expansion.
