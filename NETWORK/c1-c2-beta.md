# CL1 Before CL2 — Clarification and Intervention

**BETA — visual twin of the [CL1-CL2 page]({{ '/NETWORK/c1-c2/' | relative_url }}) with illustrative figures under selected paragraphs.** This twin tests whether illustration belongs on reference pages; if it works, the figures can be promoted into the original, and this twin can be deleted.

**BETA — source-based editorial synthesis**

The existing Lexicon defines **CL1** as correspondence-oriented clarification when something materially relevant remains unresolved. **CL2** covers correction, caution, steering, warning, or other intervention. Their ordering matters when the object has not yet been sufficiently established.

![CL1 before CL2: clarify first, then intervene only where the object is still unresolved.]({{ '/assets/beta/c1-c2/fig-order-c1-before-c2.jpg' | relative_url }})

*Figure 1 — The ordering is the point: CL1 (clarify) comes before CL2 (intervene), and CL2 only where CL1 shows the object is still unresolved.*

## John's orientation: CL1 is clarifying questions, not a verdict {#c1-not-a-verdict}

> CL1 is never wrong because it is not about right or wrong ... study the true meaning of the word clarifying questions (not matter what answer, IF it clarifies it dd job what it suppose to do!)

Spelling Δ from the source quote: “no matter”; “did its job”; “supposed to do.” The terminology is updated to CL1.

CL1 is a **clarifying question**. It is not a right/wrong score. If it clarifies, it did its job — whatever the answer. Treating a CL1 as a verdict that was “lost” or “won” substitutes scoring for clarification.

![A clarifying question as light on the object: an opened window, not a gavel.]({{ '/assets/beta/c1-c2/fig-c1-clarifying-question.jpg' | relative_url }})

*Figure 2 — A clarifying question opens light on the object. Because it is not a verdict, it cannot be lost or won.*

## A specific unresolved point

A CL1 move identifies the ambiguity that matters to the next step. It leaves room for the answer to change the evaluation. Questions that already assume guilt, error, authority abuse, or harmlessness can import a conclusion while appearing to clarify.

## Outnuancing connection

John’s source says CL2 may use frame-auditing methods while omitting the deep ethical process inseparable from Outnuancing. The audit therefore includes the proposed intervention, its assumptions, and its effect on the source object.

The opening conversation provides a worked case: an AI introduces an authority-structure concern; John asks how it follows and clarifies the mutually corrective relation; the AI revises its account. The sequence can be examined without treating the AI’s explanation of its internal causes as direct access to those causes.
