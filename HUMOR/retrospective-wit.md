## Retrospective wit — the mutual joy of improving {#retrospective-wit}

John Kuhles calls it **retrospective wit**: the punchline makes you look back at how you understood the setup. His formula describes why a sense of humor can be a key to Deep Ethics. The willingness to learn is what lets the discovery improve the listener, the joke-teller, or both.

1. **Postpone judgment.** Let the setup remain open.
2. **Have the patience to wait for the punchline.** Give the relationship time to become visible.
3. **Keep several perspectives and situations available for comparison.** An early reading can work provisionally without taking over the whole scene.
4. **Look back once the punchline lands.** [Thinking, Consciousness, and Awareness (TCoAw™)]({{ '/NETWORK/tcoaw/#retrospective-awareness-in-use' | relative_url }}) describes this retrospective shift in what you notice.
5. **Value the new insight or wisdom.** Part of the joy is seeing what becomes possible with the better reading.
6. **Let your own partial miss come into view.** The joke can lay bare an unchecked “looks like” or “sounds like”—including yours.
7. **Keep the counter-wit open to the joke-teller.** The joke is meant to have fun with the insight and leave the inquiry open. The people involved do not have to stay in fixed roles as the clever one and the one being corrected.

### “I did not searched John :P”

GPT first said it did not know how many people had brought the elements of John's formula together so explicitly. John teased it with the narrower question of what is publicly findable through multiple search engines. GPT refined the scope and acknowledged it had not run the comparison, then explained how to vary search terms. John's punchline put the plain admission in GPT's own voice:

<div class="accm-rounded-quote" markdown="1">

> I don’t know how many people PUBLICLY have brought those elements together this explicitly FINADFABLE via multiple different search engines, because I did not searched John :P
>
> — John Kuhles 🦜🦋🌳, teasing GPT by voicing the reply

</div>

The punchline was about what GPT had actually done. No search comparison had been run. Public findability is itself a question about search terms, indexing and the language different authors use. It cannot settle what people have practiced without publishing. John recognized a familiar response pattern from his own extended A.I. testing and kept open the possibility that another response might surprise him.

GPT initially made the joy sound like the payoff for an individual's successful correction. John caught the flattening, then refined his own correction to restore the mutual relation:

<div class="accm-rounded-quote" markdown="1">

> you flattened the original mutual trajectory of joy of improving :D
>
> — John Kuhles 🦜🦋🌳

</div>

Picture people joking in a room in 1980: they could already practice all seven moves with one another. What extends from individual to group psychology is whether people can change their minds without losing standing, including the person who set up the joke. The joy can begin while the possibilities are still open and deepen when someone sees what they missed. For an A.I., the observable question is whether the insight changes its next response.
