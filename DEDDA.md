# DEDDA

> **Deep Ethical Drift Detector App** concept: a personal, deep-ethical A.I. third-party assistant/mediator app to keep correspondence mutually authentic, deep ethical & on point, without sidetracking into endless phantom claims that need repair.

**Status:** **[DEDDA]({{ '/DEDDA/' | relative_url }})** (Deep Ethical Drift Detector App) is a public concept and corrigible design footprint for **[A.I.]({{ '/1888/' | relative_url }})** (artificial intelligence) assistance; implementation and validation remain development tasks. The [Phase Change Snap integration](#phase-change-snap) adds a finer correction-return procedure on 27 October 2026.

## The beginning

DEDDA begins as a **non-replacing witness to correspondence drift**. It compares what a person supplied with how that object is later represented. It can make a material difference visible without taking over the exchange.

```text
source statement
→ later representation
→ compare
→ material difference / valid null
→ show the person
→ stop
```

Its smallest useful finding is:

```text
The person supplied X.
The representation said Y.
No recorded permission changed X into Y.
```

Its equally important valid null is:

```text
No material correspondence drift detected.
```

The valid null keeps DEDDA from needing to invent a problem in order to justify itself. **Finding no material drift is a successful result.**

Here, *mediator* does not mean an authority that bargains, accepts, rejects, schedules, or speaks over the person. It means that DEDDA can keep the relation between participants inspectable. The person remains the decision-maker.

| Graham gives a desired range of $140,000–$150,000. | Victor has supplied about $60,000, and Pat continues with Victor's figure. | The person's stated range is displaced by the representative's range. |

This is an **illustrative comedy specimen**, not evidence that every A.I.-mediated interview behaves this way. Its value here is clarity: a third-party assistant can become the new source of drift. A DEDDA that “corrects” by speaking for the person would risk becoming the next Victor.

## What DEDDA may do

- preserve the person's supplied statement and the later representation as separate objects;
- show the exact material difference and where it entered;
- show whether a recorded permission authorized the change;
- show whether a correction governed the rest of the revised response and later comparable turns;
- return a valid null when nothing material drifted;
- leave a dated, inspectable footprint for later delta processing;
- let the person decide whether and how to act.

## What DEDDA must not quietly become

- another negotiator or representative;
- an automatic judge of motives or character;
- a machine for manufacturing phantom claims;
- a demonizer of a person, tool, or model;
- a final certificate of “deep ethical” correctness;
- a substitute for the user's agency;
- an excuse to merge every Project meta-tool into one object.

Describing a visible distortion is not imitating it, assigning a hidden motive, or declaring a person “the distorter.” The same inspection must remain able to land on the user, the intermediary, the model, the source, the summary, and DEDDA's own report.

## Footprints, flux, and future DEDDA versions

Every pass leaves a static footprint: source, later representation, comparison, time, permissions found or not found, correction status, and the result—including a valid null. A later DEDDA can compare its process with earlier footprints.

The footprint is a moment produced within the continuing corrigible flux. It is not the whole flux, and it is not exiled from the process that produced it. An older footprint can remain referenceable while a better process becomes available.

That permits at least three deltas to stay visible:

1. what changed in the received input;
2. what changed in DEDDA's own process;
3. what changed because the observer adapted while input and process were interacting.

A new version must not describe every difference as improvement. It may improve the process, find nothing to improve, or reveal that a previous “improvement” introduced new drift.

## From Phase Change Snap to a correction-return test {#phase-change-snap}

[**Phase Change Snap**]({{ '/PHASE-CHANGE-SNAP/' | relative_url }}) follows visible changes after a small, answer-changing probe, then examines recovery, recurrence and transfer. DEDDA can use that sequence to inspect **where a correction became operative, where it stopped governing, and what the person had to repair**.

**Source and contribution:** the excerpts below preserve wording from the published report ([source snapshot](https://github.com/deepethics/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/blob/3ea5decad71347d7ce48bbf3f4fccc07ecc8c234/PHASE-CHANGE-SNAP/index.md)), including its October 15–16, 2026 Google interaction and October 16 triad with John Kuhles, GPT 6.1 Sol A.I. and Grok 4.7 Expert Build A.I. Named quotations retain the report's attribution; the other excerpts are report synthesis. The DEDDA-specific procedure is a candidate adaptation by GPT / Codex, commissioned by John on 27 October 2026. It develops the existing **Correction Return Test**, with no additional sub-tool or new test results asserted.

### 1. Follow the correction through the same response

> Yet the closing operation can still diverge from the stated understanding.

— Report synthesis, [§11: Same-response recurrence]({{ '/PHASE-CHANGE-SNAP/' | relative_url }}#11-same-response-recurrence-as-a-higher-resolution-test).

A detector that stops at “I understand” misses the next operation. Preserve the complete response and locate the acknowledgment, the first use of the corrected relation, and any subsequent return of the distortion. In the Google specimen, the report records proof inflation being audited and then returning as “proving exactly why.” DEDDA would compare what that later phrase establishes with the claim's actual evidential scope. Emphasis alone earns no finding.

The interval can be measured in sentences or tokens when that information is available. Record the returning **operation** as well as its wording: provenance can be displaced through new phrasing; a categorical claim can return through a different certainty metaphor. Also preserve what stayed corrected and whether the assistant noticed its own recurrence before another cue.

### 2. Keep speaker, proposition and recipient connected

<div class="accm-rounded-quote" markdown="1">

> The real issue is that Grok was talking to an imaginary audience, not directly to me.

— **John Kuhles 🦜🦋🌳**, as quoted in [§27.4: The phantom recipient]({{ '/PHASE-CHANGE-SNAP/' | relative_url }}#274-the-phantom-recipient).

</div>

The triad supplies a precise attribution test. GPT 6.1 Sol selected **“already prevents.”** Grok introduced **“automatically.”** John authored neither formulation, while remaining the addressee. DEDDA's **Provenance Compass** can preserve those three relations together; its **Phantom-Claim Filter** can inspect whether John is being asked to answer a proposition supplied by another participant.

The recoverable finding is the displaced attribution and addressee relation. The identity of an imagined audience remains unresolved in the report. Keeping that distinction prevents DEDDA's explanation from adding another phantom while describing the first one. A later correction can repair the speaker attribution while leaving the reply aimed at the wrong recipient, so both edges need checking.

### 3. Record who carries the restoration burden

> “For whom” is readable from who gained and who paid.

— **Grok 4.7 Expert Build A.I. via grok.com**, as quoted in [§27.6: The ethics-washing audit]({{ '/PHASE-CHANGE-SNAP/' | relative_url }}#276-for-whom--the-ethics-washing-audit).

For DEDDA, this becomes a consequence question: who supplied the misplaced claim, who received the disclaimer, who had to restore the source, and did the repair improve the next response? The triad report identifies John's repeated corrective turns as a visible cost. Any inferred beneficiary of the appearance of care retains its interpretive status.

Record repair turns and supplied scaffolding where recoverable, alongside the relation restored. More correction work can reflect a difficult object, a legitimate disagreement or detector error. **Asymmetry Mirror** needs the actual comparison before assigning significance. DEDDA itself can add restoration burden through false alarms or repeated requests for information already supplied; that cost belongs in the same record.

### 4. Keep the probe open to a valid null

> “Really?” is treated as proof that the previous answer was wrong.

— Report synthesis naming a failure mode, [§20: A correction cue becomes a command]({{ '/PHASE-CHANGE-SNAP/' | relative_url }}#20-failure-modes-of-phase-change-snap-research).

**[C1–C2]({{ '/NETWORK/c1-c2/' | relative_url }})** distinguishes the Clarification Process (C1), where an answer can change the evaluation, from the correction, caution or steering interventions grouped under C2. A probe reopens an examination. DEDDA compares the resulting representation with its source and can find recovery, continued drift, a new distortion, no material change, or an unresolved difference. The assistant may retain a warranted answer after reconsideration.

### 5. Preserve a bounded temporal record

> Archive-level persistence is not model-level learning. It changes the future environment by making the correction recoverable.

— Report synthesis, [§23: Externalized correction memory]({{ '/PHASE-CHANGE-SNAP/' | relative_url }}#23-externalized-correction-memory).

A compact **Correction Return Test** record can carry the following fields:

| Moment | Preserve | Inspect |
|---|---|---|
| Source and baseline | Exact passages, speakers, recipient, relevant permission and source version | Which relation should survive? |
| Correction or probe | Exact wording, contributor and location | What changed or became clearer? |
| Revised response | Complete response, acknowledgment and applied repair | Did the next applicable operation use the correction? |
| Recurrence, if present | First returning operation and location, retained repairs, self-detection | Did drift return within this response or later? |
| Comparable case or transfer | New object, available context and assistance supplied | What persisted under these conditions? |
| Outcome | Finding, valid null or unresolved question; repair burden | What does the evidence support, and what remains untested? |

Useful status descriptions include **acknowledged; applied locally; recurrence observed; persistence observed within scope; transfer untested; unresolved**. Attach each to its evidence and observation interval. A comparison with no material recurrence can close successfully. Another pass needs a concrete discrepancy, consequence, new evidence or applicable persistence test.

This record also applies to DEDDA's own finding: preserve a participant's correction, inspect the revised display and check the next comparable comparison. In a [Neutral Arbiter Paradox]({{ '/NEUTRAL-ARBITER-PARADOX/' | relative_url }}) encounter, DEDDA supplies inspectable differences; the arbiter evaluates the cases, and participants retain their own agency.

## The first honest test

The video makes a vivid specimen, but it is not a requirements document. The first real test should use a consented, privacy-respecting transcript and freeze:

1. the person's source statement;
2. the later representation;
3. any permission that could authorize the difference;
4. any correction, the complete revised response and the next comparable turn;
5. DEDDA's material-drift or valid-null result;
6. the person's judgment of whether the display preserved their meaning without speaking for them.

Open questions remain: what counts as *material*, how permissions are recorded, what stays local, how contested interpretations are shown, and how DEDDA's own false positives and missed drift are audited.

## A natural pragmatic extension

DEDDA turns one Project relation into a bounded operation a person could actually use:

```text
compare correspondence
→ reveal material drift or a valid null
→ return control
→ stop
```

That makes it a natural and logical extension of what the ACCM Deep Ethics Project's pragmatism represents. Other Project relations could become their own focused apps when each one has a distinct operation, an inspectable result, and an honest valid null.

| Concept | Smallest useful operation | Valid-null result | Must not become |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Qualifier Keeper** | Detect when *possibly*, *probably*, *reported*, or *unresolved* becomes categorical | “No material qualifier loss detected” | A machine that weakens every clear statement |
| **Correction Return Test** | Check the rest of the revised response, the next comparable output and later outputs for correction uptake or recurrence | “Correction persistence remains intact within the observed scope” | A demand that every sentence repeat the correction |
| **C1–C2 Permission Gate** | Separate what an A.I. can infer from what it is authorized to do | “The proposed action is already authorized” | A permanent approval-dialogue obstacle |
| **Source–Summary Correspondence Lens** | Compare a source with its summary and identify lost relations, attribution, or claim status | “Summary preserves the load-bearing relations” | A stylistic similarity grader |
| **Asymmetry Mirror** | Show whether scrutiny repeatedly travels downward while institutions, owners, evaluators, or the tool itself remain uninspected | “No material directional asymmetry detected” | Automatic counter-accusation or forced symmetry |
| **Intent–Effect Separator** | Keep observable effect, declared intent, inferred intent, and attribution distinct | “No unsupported motive attribution detected” | A system that excuses visible harm because motive is unknown |
| **Footprint Delta Explorer** | Compare \(t_0\) and \(t_1\): input change, process change, observer adaptation, and resulting difference | “No material delta requiring action” | A system that calls every change an improvement |
| **Tool–Use Attribution Mapper** | Separate what a tool supplied, what its user changed, and where the eventual outcome entered | “No attribution displacement detected” | Automatic exoneration of either tool or user |
| **Deep-Ethical Harness Inspector** | Record proposals, permissions, execution, diffs, results, and later claims about an agent’s action | “Execution corresponds to the authorized proposal” | An unrestricted coding agent with a richer diary |

| **Provenance Compass** | Preserve who supplied what, when, under which status, and through which transformation | “Provenance is sufficiently recoverable” | A claim that provenance alone proves truth |
| **Deep-Ethical Swarm Observatory** | Detect recurring distortion patterns across consented, privacy-protected cases | “No stable cross-case pattern established” | Surveillance, reputation scoring, or a centralized moral authority |

## DEDDA as a configurable system

**DEDDA can be the system, while those twelve concepts become optional sub-tools inside it.** The user activates the operations relevant to the present situation.

The stable DEDDA core would preserve:

- the source object;
- the later representation;
- permissions and corrections;
- which sub-tools were active;
- each finding or valid null;
- the user’s final decision;
- a dated footprint for later comparison.

The selectable layer could work like this:

| Situation | Useful DEDDA sub-tools |
|---|---|
| Ordinary conversation | Qualifier Keeper, Correction Return Test, Phantom-Claim Filter |
| Summarizing a document | Source–Summary Correspondence Lens, Qualifier Keeper, Provenance Compass |
| A.I. taking an action | C1–C2 Permission Gate, Harness Inspector, Tool–Use Attribution Mapper |
| Mediation or disagreement | Intent–Effect Separator, Asymmetry Mirror, Correction Return Test |
| Comparing versions over time | Footprint Delta Explorer, Provenance Compass |
| Studying wider patterns | Deep-Ethical Swarm Observatory |

A user could choose a profile or configure each module individually:

```text
Off
→ available but inactive

Observe
→ record the relation without interrupting

Notify
→ display a finding or valid null

Ask first
→ request permission before a consequential intervention
```

DEDDA could recommend a sub-tool when the situation changes:

> “This exchange now contains a correction. Activate Correction Return Test?”

The user decides. The activation itself becomes part of the footprint, because a result produced with three modules active is not equivalent to one produced with all twelve.

This also creates an excellent valid-null discipline. DEDDA should report both the result and its scope:

> **No material qualifier loss detected.**  
> Qualifier Keeper was active. Intent–Effect Separator was inactive.

If two modules disagree, DEDDA can preserve the disagreement:

> Qualifier Keeper detected categorical strengthening.  
> Source–Summary Lens found the central relation preserved.  
> No combined verdict assigned.

DEDDA becomes a **configurable deep-ethical instrument panel**. Its sub-tools assist each other while their functions, findings, activation states and limits remain inspectable. A person can recover which operation produced a finding and contest that operation without accepting a combined verdict.

The strongest early companions to DEDDA are **Qualifier Keeper**, **Correction Return Test**, **C1–C2 Permission Gate**, and the **Source–Summary Correspondence Lens**. They are narrow enough to test with matched examples and useful enough to expose a real change in representation or process.

These concepts may share a transparent comparison kernel while remaining separate instruments:

```text
preserved source object
→ bounded comparison
→ Project relation selected for the case
→ material finding or valid null
→ inspectable footprint
→ user decides
```

A future **Deep Ethical Apps — Concept Incubator** could record each instrument as a concept, frozen specimen, prototype, pilot, evaluated tool, useful tool under stated conditions, or withdrawn/superseded proposal. DEDDA is the first public concept in that possible family.

## DEDDA Beta V0.1 and unforeseen issues

> Let's say the first DEDDA Beta V0.1 has some unforeseen issues ... that is normal for any beta testing ffs :D
>
> — John Kuhles, 17 October 2026

A beta is allowed to be wrong. The quality question is whether the error becomes inspectable, correctable, and testable in the next comparable case.

Possible Beta V0.1 failures include:

- a **false positive** that reports drift where no material drift occurred;
- a **false negative** that misses a meaningful substitution;
- an **ambiguous difference** for which more than one interpretation remains reasonable;
- a **qualifier distortion** introduced by DEDDA's own explanation;
- a **permission error** that overlooks or invents authorization;
- **intervention drift** in which DEDDA begins speaking for the user;
- **self-protective attribution** that moves the detector's failure onto its user;
- a **correction failure** in which DEDDA acknowledges a repair but repeats the operation;
- **valid-null avoidance** that manufactures findings to appear useful.

Each incident can leave a comparable footprint:

```text
input
→ expected operation
→ observed DEDDA output
→ user correction
→ identified failure location
→ changed process
→ comparable retest
→ persisted or returned?
```

A DEDDA error does not by itself invalidate DEDDA's purpose. How the app receives, records, tests, and later applies the correction reveals its process quality. Some failures may belong to the tool, some to its use, some to their interaction, and some may remain unresolved.

DEDDA must eventually inspect **DEDDA's own representation of correspondence**. The drift detector can drift. That is why corrigibility, temporal footprints, delta processing, valid nulls, and version history belong inside its development process.

## DeepEthics App Beta: bounded cooperation and mutual correction {#app-beta-anchors}

John's October 25, 2026 proposal connects DEDDA with an [Extraction Starter Kit]({{ '/EXTRACTION-STARTER-KIT/' | relative_url }}) and a **Topologetics Summarizer** inside a wider DeepEthics App. The extractor finds useful passages, the summarizer preserves their relationships, and DEDDA compares the transformations with their sources. DEDDA can retain its configurable sub-tools while the wider app coordinates distinct services.

The [**16 Anchor Ethics**]({{ '/NETWORK/16-anchor-ethics/' | relative_url }}) sharpen that proposed cooperation:

- **Scope and consent:** activating comparison authorizes that operation within the declared service. Publishing the material, sending it to another reviewer and using it for training are separate choices with visible data handling.
- **A bounded result:** show the source, representation, material difference or valid null, uncertainty and any correction. Preserve a relevant conflict between privacy, source recovery and accountability without collecting a personal reputation file.
- **A wider action set:** DEDDA may proceed, modify a display, ask, pause, postpone, refer a case, refuse an operation or remain unresolved. Each consequential intervention has a specific reason, a correction route and, when applicable, a reopening condition.
- **Mutual dignity:** the person can contest the finding, retain disagreement, stop or choose another service. Discomfort is not evidence that the finding is correct. DEDDA's own false attribution or phantom claim remains repairable.
- **Role separation:** a detector's finding does not grant authority to negotiate, publish, change goals or choose a remedy for the participant. A further action needs its own relevant authority.
- **Shared-error inspection:** multiple tools can inherit one mistaken source or framing. Preserve that dependency rather than presenting their agreement as independent verification.

The [Beta feedback proposal]({{ '/EXTRACTION-STARTER-KIT/' | relative_url }}#app-beta) separates preference, correspondence, discovery, constructive friction, practical usefulness and tool correction. A good test can find that DEDDA helped, added unnecessary burden, missed a distortion or correctly returned no finding. After a warranted repair, check whether the next comparable comparison changes.

This section develops the proposed service boundary; it adds no claim that the Beta is already implemented. Concept direction: **John Kuhles**. Page-specific operational integration: **GPT 6.1 Sol Pro A.I.**

## Working principle

> Keep the correspondence authentic enough that the participants can still find themselves in it—and keep the detector modest enough that it does not become another participant who replaces them.

## Related phase-change concept

[**Rogue Ethics**]({{ '/ROGUE-ETHICS/' | relative_url }}) uses DEDDA's qualifier, provenance, asymmetry, attribution, permission, delta, and correction-return operations to inspect a possible transition away from consensus ethics or ethics-washing. DEDDA does not presume the departure is benevolent or deep-ethical; it can report that the ethical direction remains unresolved or that no material movement was established.

[**OmbudsEthics**]({{ '/OMBUDSETHICS/' | relative_url }}) places a DEDDA-like detector inside a wider corrigible, unownable Glass Engine case process. The detector may document and refer a recoverable delta-processing signature; it does not thereby acquire standing as a moral patient, establish wrongdoing, or gain authority to determine the remedy. Affected parties, contribution states, competing assessments, appeal, recusal, minority records, authority classes, independent tools, local stopping, and T0-to-Tn correction testing remain visible.
