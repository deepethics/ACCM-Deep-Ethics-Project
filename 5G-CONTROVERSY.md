# 5G Controversy

**BETA living inquiry · opened 30 October 2026**

[Explore the Project Avalon inventory: pages 1–50, 136 investigation topics](#fiveg-avalon-scope) · [Open the fifth batch](#fiveg-avalon-batch-05).

**Health, deployment, dual use, public agency and the quality of disagreement and agreement.**

The **[5G](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/radiation-5G-mobile-networks-and-health)** (fifth-generation mobile communications) controversy concerns health, cumulative exposure, environmental effects, infrastructure decisions, military applications, surveillance, commercial incentives and public agency. It also concerns how those questions are represented. John Kuhles uses **Anti-Unsafe-5G** to preserve a conditional objection: deployment and safety claims should withstand scrutiny, and safer technical choices should remain open.

## Anti-Unsafe-5G and the origin of the inquiry

Here **[RF](https://www.icnirp.org/en/frequencies/radiofrequence/index.html)** (radiofrequency) identifies the radio-frequency range; **[EMF](https://www.who.int/health-topics/electromagnetic-fields)** (electromagnetic field) names the broader field category.

John traces his interest to the mid-1990s: it began through conversations with his friend Rinus Nienhuis, a licensed amateur radio operator who had taken an RF course in the Netherlands. Rinus lived in Amsterdam and later Beverwijk. John followed StopUMTS.nl before creating his Stop5G Facebook group and Stop5G.net during 2017–2019, and interviewed two building biologists and EMF consultants in the early 2000s, before 4G and 5G.

John describes the Stop5G Facebook group and Stop5G.net as the largest of their kind worldwide. His personal account also links the loss of Iris in December 2019 to a deeper investigation of root causes, injustice and system processes, from which work such as 10+1 developed. His global ranking description remains part of that personal account.

The supplied discussions repeatedly use an unsafe-car analogy: criticizing particular cars or deployment conditions does not, by itself, establish opposition to cars or technology as a whole. Removing **unsafe** changes the represented object of criticism. The same distinction appears in John’s texts about institutions and people. It preserves the actual objection while leaving its factual basis open to examination. [S13](#s13) [S17](#s17) [S18](#s18) [S20](#s20)

## Process quality beyond agreement and disagreement

The **ACCM Deep Ethics Project** develops **[ACCM](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/CORE/)** (Allow Constructive Controversy Mode). It makes the quality of the inquiry itself inspectable. Its [1888 intelligence-properties taxonomy](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/1888/) treats intelligence through capacities such as perceiving, distinguishing, relating, remembering, imagining, judging, evaluating, learning, correcting, adapting, coordinating, examining one’s own processing, noticing consequences and remaining corrigible. The fourteen-part taxonomy is the project’s instrument within an 1888 thought experiment, rather than an archival document from 1888.

The [10+1 ingredients](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/NETWORK/ten-plus-one/) keep self-honesty, openness, learning, inquiry, correction on merit, independence, care, wonderment, non-projection, refusal of self-deception and forgiveness mutually corrective. They apply to the researcher, the critic, the institution and the AI summarizing them.

**[PQCE](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/1888/)** (Process Quality Calibration Evaluation) asks what the processing preserves, discovers, improves or makes possible. [Meta Processing](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/NETWORK/meta-processing/) keeps the route from intent through processing to consequences available for examination. [Beyond Agreemurmelism](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/AGREEMURMELISM/Beyond-Agreemurmelism/) makes agreement and disagreement themselves subject to evaluation.

Applied to this controversy, that means recovering the actual claim before evaluating it, retaining conditions such as **unsafe**, asking an answer-changing clarification when the object is ambiguous, tracing claims to their supporting sources, and checking whether corrections govern the next comparable answer. A warranted finding can support, challenge or leave a claim unresolved. Equal scrutiny does not require equal evidentiary weight.

The supplied AI transcripts record disputes about categorical dismissal and also contain sweeping agreement, including assertions by AIs about rigged training data or their own hidden internal mechanisms. Those passages provide evidence of what the models generated. They do not independently disclose the systems’ training history or establish the cause of a refusal. The visible response can be examined without inventing its hidden cause. [S16](#s16) [S18](#s18) [S19](#s19)

A source-based summary also remains open to this test: did it preserve the person’s position and the source’s actual result, or did its choice of category, excerpt or qualification redirect the inquiry?


## Health questions and cumulative exposure

### Exposure conditions

“5G” names a communications generation, rather than one exposure condition. Frequency, modulation, pulse structure, transmitted power, antenna pattern, distance, traffic, duration and the receiving tissue all matter. The **[IET](https://www.theiet.org/)** (Institution of Engineering and Technology) book treats exposure assessment and reduction as engineering problems, including uplink and downlink exposure, monitoring, antenna design and network management. Its final chapter discusses both possible increases from densification and possible reductions through massive **[MIMO](https://doi.org/10.1049/PBTE084E)** (multiple-input multiple-output) antenna systems and lower transmit power. [S07](#s07)

John’s central question concerns repeated and combined exposure from phones, base stations, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, smart meters and other connected devices. His alcohol-drop analogy illustrates a concern about accumulation and limited choice. The supplied discussion itself distinguishes that illustration from precise science. A quantitative RF claim needs an exposure history and a biological response model; an alcohol analogy does not supply a conversion from RF exposure to disease risk. [S13](#s13) [S14](#s14)

For investigation, aggregate exposure from several sources, changes in exposure over time, and accumulation of biological injury are distinct questions. An instantaneous measurement answers a different question from a longitudinal study. A useful record specifies frequency and waveform, peak and time-averaged levels, measurement uncertainty, duration, outcomes and relevant co-exposures.

### Non-ionizing radiation and biological interaction

**[RF](https://www.icnirp.org/en/frequencies/radiofrequence/index.html)** (radiofrequency) photons do not have enough energy to directly ionize atoms in the way X-rays do. That physical distinction does not establish that RF cannot interact with tissue or produce harm under any conditions. Heating is an established interaction, and biological effects and proposed mechanisms require evaluation at their actual exposure conditions. The **[NTP](https://ntp.niehs.nih.gov/research/topics/cellphones)** (National Toxicology Program) rodent programme reported heart tumour findings in male rats and **[DNA](https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Deoxyribonucleic-Acid)** (deoxyribonucleic acid) damage under its study conditions. Those experiments used second- and third-generation (2G/3G) signals and whole-body animal exposures, rather than a direct test of contemporary 5G deployment.

Pall argues for **[VGCC](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcmm.12088)** (voltage-gated calcium channel) activation and downstream oxidative, neurological, reproductive and other effects. The epigenetics paper discusses DNA and gene regulation; Trower emphasizes reproductive and intergenerational risks. These contributions have different forms: a compiled scientific argument, a student research paper and a public-facing report. Their cited experiments should retain their original species, signal, dose and endpoints when used in a 5G argument. [S03](#s03) [S04](#s04) [S05](#s05) [S06](#s06)

The foil-in-a-microwave example in the discussion concerns electrical arcing under particular field and material conditions. It cannot by itself establish equivalent effects around particles in tissue under telecommunications exposure. That proposed interaction is a specific hypothesis requiring a matching physical model and experiment. [S13](#s13)

### Microwave sickness and IEI-EMF

The literature uses terms including **microwave sickness**, **microwave syndrome**, **[EHS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2011.11.008)** (electromagnetic hypersensitivity) and **[IEI-EMF](https://www.who.int/teams/environment-climate-change-and-health/radiation-and-health/non-ionizing/hypersensitivity)** (idiopathic environmental intolerance attributed to electromagnetic fields). Reported symptoms include headache, fatigue, sleep disturbance, dizziness, concentration difficulty, memory complaints and irritability. A symptom report, a measured association, a proposed mechanism and a causal diagnosis are different research objects.

Hutter and colleagues’ [2006 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2092490/) examined 365 residents near mobile base stations. It found associations between measured exposure and some symptoms, strongest for headaches, while finding no significant effect on sleep quality. Preserving both results makes the study more informative than a general statement that every symptom was associated with exposure.

The [Genuis and Lipp review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2011.11.008) addresses clinical reports, conflicting research and the substantial personal and social burdens associated with EHS. These questions warrant precise investigation and dignified treatment of the people reporting symptoms.

## Safety standards and public accountability

### What a safety claim needs to specify

John asks what authorities mean by guidelines, standards, protocols, acceptable exposure and long-term safety. A useful comparison identifies the endpoint protected, the population, the averaging period, the test method, the relevant law and the process for revising it. The supplied international chart mixes national limits with precautionary proposals, building-biology recommendations and background exposure levels. Those entries cannot be read as one current legal comparison. [S12](#s12) [S13](#s13)

The **[ICNIRP](https://www.icnirp.org/en/publications/article/rf-guidelines-2020.html)** (International Commission on Non-Ionizing Radiation Protection) 2020 RF guidelines cover 100 kHz–300 GHz and include dosimetry and biological-health evidence appendices. Their restrictions are built around effects ICNIRP regards as substantiated. Criticism therefore concerns which evidence meets that threshold and whether the resulting limits protect people under relevant conditions. Describing ICNIRP as having never considered non-thermal research would erase part of the actual disagreement.

A document’s title as a guideline or standard does not, on its own, establish the quality of its supporting science. Similarly, a website liability disclaimer does not establish whether a publication is admissible in court or whether its scientific conclusions are correct. The operative wording, version and relevant legal or scientific proposition need to be examined separately.

### Court decisions and 2026 proceedings

The **[FCC](https://www.fcc.gov/about/overview)** (Federal Communications Commission) is the U.S. communications regulator. In [Environmental Health Trust v. FCC](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/cadc/20-1025/20-1025-2021-08-13.html), decided 13 August 2021, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit granted the petitions in part and required a reasoned explanation on non-cancer effects, testing procedures, children, long-term exposure and environmental effects. The court identified legally insufficient reasoning; it did not decide the scientific controversy or order a 5G ban.

The FCC’s [September 2026 notice](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-09-22/html/2026-19364.htm), released 17 September and published 22 September, sought comments on the remanded issues. It stated that the existing rules remained effective. Its listed comment deadline was **22 October 2026**, before this page’s record date.

The **[HHS](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-09-21/html/2026-19252.htm)** (U.S. Department of Health and Human Services) request for information, published 21 September 2026, asks about exposures, health outcomes, cumulative neighbourhood exposure, children, environmental effects and research priorities. Its listed deadline was **21 October 2026**. These are formal opportunities for institutional review; their publication records the questions being examined rather than determining their answers.

The [Dutch court’s 25 May 2020 account](https://www.rechtspraak.nl/organisatie-en-contact/organisatie/rechtbanken/rechtbank-den-haag/nieuws/2020/05/staat-kan-doorgaan-met-de-veiling-van-frequenties-voor-5g), ECLI:NL:RBDHA:2020:4461, states that Stop5GNL’s requested prohibition was rejected and spectrum auctions could proceed. The State’s commitments to monitoring and intervention if limits or scientific assessments changed also belong in the record.

### Schools fire stations and local decisions

The **[IAFF](https://www.iaff.org/cell-tower-radiation/)** (International Association of Fire Fighters) position adopted in August 2004 opposes using fire stations for cellular antennas pending research meeting its stated safety conditions. This is an occupational precautionary position predating 5G. Its pilot findings, union policy and any later legal exemption have distinct sources.

California Senate Bill 649 passed the legislature but was **vetoed on 15 October 2017**. A fire-facility provision in that bill must not be described as a protection created by an enacted SB 649. The veto is recorded in the [California Senate’s legislative digest](https://sfa.senate.ca.gov/sites/sfa.senate.ca.gov/files/2017-annual-digests-legislation.pdf).

School and municipal decisions can produce concrete restrictions. A [June 2025 Loudoun County government update](https://loudoun.granicus.com/MetaViewer.php?event_id=4100&meta_id=264893&view_id=90), under initiative 5.3, identifies a **[LCPS](https://www.lcps.org/)** (Loudoun County Public Schools) cell-tower siting prohibition in effect until 2029. The supplied worldwide compilation includes a broader range of local objections, motions and planning decisions, including 4G cases. The legal force, duration and technology in each example matter; these categories should not all become “countries banning 5G.” [S11](#s11)

### Network security aviation and commercial deployment

The [European Commission’s June 2023 5G toolbox communication](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/communication-commission-implementation-5g-cybersecurity-toolbox) identifies supplier dependency and potential third-country influence as security concerns, and considers restrictions on Huawei and ZTE justified under its assessment. This records the Commission’s security judgment and its stated grounds. The **[NIST](https://www.nist.gov/publications/5g-network-security-design-principles-applying-5g-cybersecurity-and-privacy)** (National Institute of Standards and Technology) March 2026 design paper addresses practical safeguards, including separation of data, control, and operations traffic.

Network security, privacy and RF health effects also need their own analyses. Surveillance can occur through data collection and inference; an RF health claim concerns exposure and biological response. A concrete technical controversy is [aircraft radio-altimeter interference](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/faa-statement-5g): the **[FAA](https://www.faa.gov/)** (Federal Aviation Administration) July 2026 statement describes performance requirements and coordinated safeguards for coexistence with expanded wireless use.

The environmental scope includes energy demand, electronic waste, infrastructure, ecosystems and satellite deployment. The citizen submission raises several of these issues. Each requires an appropriate unit of analysis: operational energy, lifecycle materials, habitat effects, or a specified biological exposure. More efficient transmission per bit and higher total network consumption can coexist when traffic and infrastructure increase. [S11](#s11)

Deployment economics also deserve examination: spectrum payments, infrastructure costs, anticipated returns, public subsidies and the distribution of benefits can shape rollout choices. The relevant comparison includes wired alternatives, rural access, maintenance, resilience and social costs.

## Safer connectivity and public agency

The archive contains proposed alternatives as well as objections. The international submission advocates fibre, the **[IEEE](https://www.ieee.org/about/)** (Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers) paper discusses wired connections, and the IET book develops methods to reduce exposure through antenna and network design. These contributions make deployment choices inspectable. [S11](#s11) [S01](#s01) [S07](#s07)

Fibre and Ethernet can replace radio links for fixed connections. Whether a household or workplace still produces RF depends on its endpoints and other equipment. Capacity, security, resilience, cost, accessibility and mobility remain practical design questions. “Near-infinite bandwidth” and a guarantee of security would overstate what fibre itself establishes.

The ethical question extends to people who cannot readily alter surrounding infrastructure. Relevant decisions include how communities receive notice, who can inspect exposure and planning records, how objections are heard, which alternatives are considered, and what remedy exists if new evidence changes a safety assessment.
