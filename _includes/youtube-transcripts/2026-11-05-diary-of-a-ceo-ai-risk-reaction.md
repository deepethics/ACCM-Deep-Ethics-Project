The closest connection is **who gets to decide which risks other people must bear**. Bartlett asks who has the right to press the button. McAfee asks who has the right to withhold benefits by stopping development. The ACCM Deep Ethics Project puts both decisions through scrutiny, including the evidence, the people affected, the authority to act, and the opportunity to reconsider. [Due Process for Ethics](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/DUE-PROCESS-FOR-ETHICS/#burdens-and-risk)

The closest link to **Categorizing Issues** is the discussion of *narrow* systems. Your island crew shows how several limited skills can produce something new through coordination. Apply that to a system that analyzes evidence, a system that plans, and a tool that can act on the plan. The combination can create useful work and new risks. **The safety question belongs to the whole arrangement**, including what the parts can do together, which permissions they have, and whether someone can interrupt the resulting process. [Categorizing Issues](https://deepethics.github.io/ACCM-Deep-Ethics-Project/CATEGORIZING-ISSUES/#island)

There’s also a live example of **C1 before C2** in the Waymo exchange. Bartlett asks McAfee what evidence would change his position. That makes a threshold discussable. Then Bartlett paraphrases the threshold as accepting an *existential* conclusion, while McAfee answers that he would probably support *legal and regulatory guardrails*. The distinction matters. The evidence someone needs to support a particular intervention can differ from what they need to accept a forecast of extinction.

I’d read the clip as the **opening entry in a V.C.R.A.S. case**. Preserve each assessment and its qualifiers, the proposed action, who bears the costs of acting or waiting, and what later evidence would change the decision. Then follow whether the institution or evaluator actually changes its next move when that evidence arrives.
