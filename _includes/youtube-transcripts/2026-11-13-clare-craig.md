> I've been asked to talk about Module Four of the Inquiry, which is on vaccines and therapeutics. That means we need to talk about what they decided beforehand could not be asked, and what they deliberately shut down during the discussions and the hearings.
>
> An incredible amount of time and money has been spent on this Inquiry, yet the reports have failed to address key scientific and ethical questions. Worse still, there were specific questions that were consciously excluded from consideration. They didn't even publish the written statements that they asked HART to submit, and other groups, and you do wonder whether they took the time to read them.
>
> So, what is a vaccine? Vaccines are sold to the public as a safe and effective way to prevent a disease by priming the body to defend against infection and therefore transmission. The COVID vaccines did not prevent infections. They did not prevent transmission. Lord James Bethell, who signed the authorisation, said at the Inquiry: it didn't stop transmission. It didn't stop long COVID. And when challenged at the dispatch box, Rishi Sunak, when asked to defend the full phrase “safe and effective,” couldn't do so.
>
> They could not prevent infection. An injection in the arm cannot produce immunity in the nose and the throat, and that's something that's been known for decades. Indeed, Fauci specifically stated this before and after, but not when it mattered.
>
> Now, some people think the COVID vaccines prevented serious illness and deaths. The data clearly shows that the vaccines did not prevent serious illness and deaths. In other words, they were not effective. And I've summarised all of that in my book *Spiked*, which is not for sale at the back, but at least it's written for posterity.
>
> Shamefully, and without any evidence, Hugo Keith KC, who is the lead counsel for the Inquiry, talked about the vaccines, describing them as entirely effective, undoubted successes, and even went as far as saying that they offered the promised land. Would he say that about any other novel pharmaceutical product? I don't think so.
>
> Worse still, the COVID vaccines were not safe. Before the hearings had even begun, the Inquiry had already decided which questions it would not answer. The Inquiry specifically ruled out in advance, and I quote, “reaching a concluded view on the safety of specific vaccines.” They said they would examine the systems designed to detect harm, but not whether the vaccines caused harm. Now, how can you judge whether a system worked without first establishing what harms it might have failed to detect?
>
> On the first morning of the vaccine module hearings, Hugo Keith warned that accounts of serious side effects, quote, “must not be used as a platform to argue that vaccination is not overwhelmingly beneficial at a population level.” The Inquiry's conclusion was determined before a word of evidence had been heard.
>
> When Charlet Crichton of the UKCV Family was on the stand, she mentioned participants in the clinical trial who had their data scrubbed because they had been injured, but he stopped her speaking, saying “we can't be looking at individual cases.” Now, that was a rule that didn't apply when the individual case had a diagnosis of COVID, but actually she hadn't even mentioned an individual case anyway. She was talking about fundamental corruption in the only reliable measure we have of vaccine safety, and the counsel for the Inquiry didn't question her about it, didn't want to know, and the chair didn't intervene either.
>
> When Ruth O’Rafferty of the Scottish Vaccine Injured Group raised the fundamental difference between the product that was used in the clinical trials and the product that was mass-produced and given to the public, she was shut down with “we don't have the time or the wherewithal.” This was a point-blank refusal to hear evidence that was unhelpful for the safe and effective narrative.
>
> But the Inquiry did find time for an extended examination of what they called vaccine misinformation. Professor Heidi Larson was asked for 105 minutes about misinformation and offered only two concrete examples: that people were being told to eat garlic and drink chlorine. Now, of course, neither of those examples has any bearing on the safety or efficacy of any vaccine.
>
> Now, if you go back to 2009, we had a so-called swine flu pandemic, which some of you might remember, and Dame Deirdre Hine was asked to, and did, write a report on it in 2010. She warned that responses must be proportionate, that worst-case scenario assumptions must not be treated as predictions, and that modellers must not be treated as court astrologers. Baroness Hallett, by contrast, made plenty of time to have those very same court astrologers in her court.
>
> The numbers they produced were extraordinary, but they depended on assumptions that the Inquiry could have scrutinised and failed to. They ended up reporting that the vaccine saved an estimated 475,000 lives in England and Scotland. The model behind that number produced a range, but the Inquiry gave only one number and acted as if these were real lives. They were cardboard-cutout fantasy lives. They could only have been real if an impossible number of people had been infected.
>
> And there were other important topics that were not mentioned, presumably for lack of time. Lipid nanoparticle risks, mortality from cardiovascular disease and sudden deaths, different dosages and arrangements of the Pfizer and Moderna vaccines, how much spike is produced in which organs and for how long — all of that was off limits.
>
> Now, amazingly, in an earlier module, Baroness Hallett had let the mask slip when she revealed her methodological bias by asking: if there's a possible benefit, what's the downside? This is not the approach of a scientist, whether she was talking about vaccines or, in that case, masks.
>
> Future generations will be appalled at what happened in 2020 and 2021. They will be appalled at the abandonment of medical ethics, at the coercion, at the failure to respect informed consent, and they will be appalled at an Inquiry established to learn the lessons that deliberately excluded some of the most fundamental questions. There was time for modellers, time for predictions about saved lives, time for misinformation, but no time to answer the most fundamental questions about these products administered to millions of people.
>
> Did they work? Were they safe? Was any of it ethical?
>
> The Hallett Inquiry has consumed extraordinary amounts of time and money, and it has been an extraordinary waste of both.

— **Dr Clare Craig**, Together speech; corrected transcript supplied by John Kuhles.
