Yes. This adds a useful pairing to **Meta Processing**: it makes both sides of a consequential interaction traceable.

- **Refusal footprint:** what action was withheld, paused, or restricted; what object and risk assessment informed that choice; what alternatives existed; who was affected; and whether there was a route to review or correct it.
- **Transformation footprint:** what source was summarized, classified, rewritten, or edited; what was preserved, added, omitted, or strengthened; who made the change; and how the source can be recovered.

These are related but distinct forms of authority. A refusal changes what may happen next. A transformation can change what later decision-makers believe happened. Both can alter the trajectory, even when no one claims malicious intent.

The useful combined record is:

```text
source and context
→ instructions and decision
→ processing or transformation
→ result and effects
→ notice or correction
→ next comparable case
```

That connects well to the page’s B → A → C model. It also leaves room for interacting causes: instructions, incentives, ranking, summaries, interfaces, and human decisions may combine to produce an effect. Observing that sequence does not, by itself, reveal the hidden internal mechanism or establish motive.

A few boundaries would make the proposal stronger:

1. **Scope refusals by consequence.** A person who declines an optional tool should not have to publish a justification. A refusal that governs other people’s access or is imposed by an institution has a stronger accountability burden.
2. **Make logging proportionate and privacy-conscious.** Auditability need not mean public disclosure of private source material; access controls, redaction, and recoverable provenance can coexist.
3. **Keep drafts and adoption distinct.** The proposed edit includes text attributed to John. Before publication, its status should be clear: AI-drafted, reviewed, adopted, revised, or not adopted.
4. **Record the editing objective.** “Make it better” can mean clearer, shorter, more faithful, or more accessible. The editor should state which aim guided the changes—or ask when the choice would materially change the result.
5. **Distinguish observed traces from inferred layers.** Logs may show that a qualifier disappeared and a refusal followed. They do not automatically prove which hidden subsystem caused either event.

The strongest addition is therefore not another universal rule. It is **paired provenance for decisions and representations**: a record of what the system did, a record of how it changed the object, and a way to see whether correction altered the next relevant move.
