# About the ACCM Deep Ethics Project

> **EARLY BETA — DRAFT — TRIAL & ERROR**

The **ACCM Deep Ethics Project** (*Allow Constructive Controversy Mode — Deep Ethics Project*) is a public, developing inquiry into what happens to an object while humans and A.I.s receive it, frame it, clarify it, classify it, evaluate it, correct it, and carry it forward.

Its recurring question is:

> **What happened to the object while you were producing the answer?**

The project studies correspondence, clarification, corrigibility, mutual dignity, process quality, mass psychology, LLM mannerisms, competing risk assessments, framing, uncertainty, and whether a correction actually changes later behavior.

It does not ask visitors to accept John Kuhles, an A.I., an institution, or the project itself as a final authority. It develops tools for keeping a high-signal object recognizable long enough to investigate it without premature endorsement, suppression, polishing, or substitution.

## Why it exists

Many systems can produce fluent conclusions while losing qualifiers, provenance, unresolved questions, anomalies, or the speaker's actual object. The ACCM Deep Ethics Project makes those transformations inspectable.

The working orientation is **omnidirectional, mutual, dignifying, high-signal, deep ethical sense-making through a process-oriented trajectory**. The same scrutiny must be able to move toward the user, the A.I., the institution, the critic, the project, and its founder.

The project is being built in public before it is finished so its sources, transformations, corrections, disagreements, and development remain visible.

## Where it came from

The project emerged from John Kuhles' decades of independent work across psychology and mass psychology, public and alternative media, controversial and suppressed-news research, long-form dialogue, and—since late 2024—thousands of hours of archived interaction with more than 100 A.I. systems.

That history is context, not a demand for belief. Read the fuller account on **[John Kuhles — Biography and Project Origins]({{ '/BIOGRAPHY/' | relative_url }})**.
