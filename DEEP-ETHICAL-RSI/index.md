# Deep Ethical RSI

**Can a city improve the way it learns from being corrected?**

In John Kuhles’s 2035 thought experiment, almost everyone in one large city uses a system that nobody owns. A vast majority voted for it. Then a resident spots what looks like misuse and comes to John with **“here is how.”** The resident already knows where to look and can show someone else what happened. The next question is what the city learns from that person, and whether a later resident can apply the same scrutiny to the repair itself.

**[RSI]({{ '/RECURSIVE-IMPROVEMENT/' | relative_url }})** (recursive self-improvement) here is a proposal about what a learning process can improve. The city’s own rules for hearing a correction, evaluating it and acting on it can learn from how they work. The case below is fictional. The decisions, dates and records give the proposal something specific to test.

## The 2035 audit desk {#audit-desk}

A service desk starts requiring people to use the city’s new tool before it accepts an application. The desk cites the city vote. A resident brings the records. **Change the ballot record first.** Then decide how far a warranted correction should travel, and whether the next resident can reach the person who can act.

### Follow the finding through three decisions {#three-decisions}

**The application.** The operative ballot text says, *“The city will make the tool available for voluntary use. People may decline it and retain access to the service.”* The North Desk adds, *“Applicants must complete the tool before their application can be accepted.”* The resident brings both texts. The departure is visible where the desk turns an offer into an entry condition. A vote tally establishes the level of support. The actual wording establishes what people were asked to authorize.

**The next office.** The city reopens the resident’s application. It then has another decision to make. It can resolve this application and leave other offices to face the same question when it next arises. Or it can change the intake procedure so the South Desk will have to show its authority before a similar condition reaches applicants. A later, comparable case at the South Desk is the test. Can another resident recover the first finding, and does the new rule govern what the second desk does?

**The review of the review.** The city adds a challenge period. A service contract can be signed on 5 June, and the notice initially opens the challenge period on 7 June. The new resident can put the dates alongside one another and show that the challenge comes after signing. Opening the period on 3 June gives people time. Giving the reviewer authority to postpone the signature lets a warranted challenge reach the actual decision. A reviewer who receives comments and can only record them changes a different part of the process.

| If the record changes | What the same inquiry can find |
| --- | --- |
| The operative ballot expressly authorizes the requirement for this service. | The intake rule matches that mandate. A resident can still challenge the policy on another stated ground. |
| Only a summary of the vote and its tally survives. | The mandate is unresolved. Recover the text, amendments and the rule the desk actually used. |
| Only the original resident’s case is reopened. | The person gets a remedy. The next desk still faces the previous intake procedure. |
| The later desk applies the new check while the decision is open. | A subsequent case supplies evidence that the repair travelled beyond the original dispute. |

This proposed exercise lets a reader vary one relationship at a time. Someone can recognize the governance problem by comparing the records, including a version where there is a valid null for mandate drift. A label such as “misuse” earns its place through a visible difference that matters to the decision.

## The resident brings the evidence before asking John {#the-resident-brings-evidence}

John’s point is that people can learn the method well enough to recognize its trajectory wherever they encounter it. **[TCoAw™]({{ '/NETWORK/tcoaw/' | relative_url }})** (Thinking, Consciousness, Awareness) gives them a way to examine their active focus and what it leaves out. In the first case the desk focuses on the vote tally. The resident recovers the omitted operative sentence. In the later case attention goes to the existence of a challenge period. Someone else notices who can act while the decision is still open.

**[PQCE™]({{ '/FLAGGING-SYSTEM/' | relative_url }})** (Process Quality Calibration Evaluation) lets people examine what happened when that missing relation returned. Did anyone change the application? Did a later office retrieve the correction and use it? Did the next review remain reachable to people affected by the rule? The **[27 obstructions and 12-stage inquiry protocol]({{ '/CORE/27-PLUS-12/' | relative_url }})** give specific places to look. For example, a published account can lose the word *voluntary* through qualifier erosion. A correction can then be acknowledged and fail to reach the next intake decision. The two failures demand different evidence and repairs.

Anyone can compare implementation with the original Deep Ethical trajectory. John’s account is first-hand evidence of what he intended and built. A resident’s records give John something to examine, and another reader can correct his comparison. **[Unownable]({{ '/UNOWNABLE/' | relative_url }})** gives the right to audit practical meaning when the next finding concerns the founders, the majority, or the person who conducted the last review. A well-supported extension can travel through the same route as a criticism.

## What the next case inherits {#what-the-next-case-inherits}

A later resident needs the actual mandate, the action taken, the correction and the actor who could use it. They need to be able to see where the authority changed and whether a person could challenge the change in time. A record saying “we improved our process” gives them far less to work with. The Cold Start Kit calls attention to the handoff from a stored artifact to **operative memory**. A later process must retrieve the relevant relation and let it govern a move. The record can also carry an unresolved dissent so later evidence can improve the assessment.

The **[16 Anchor Ethics™]({{ '/NETWORK/16-anchor-ethics/' | relative_url }})** source gives three useful inspection points. Checks and balances directs attention to who can change a decision. Asymmetric responsibility asks more of the office that can attach a new condition to an essential service. Noetic diversity keeps a citizen’s challenge in view even when the vote was overwhelming. The **[36 Truth Distortion Vectors]({{ '/DEEP-ETHICAL-RSI/context/context-04.html' | relative_url }})** add a question about the representations people end up seeing. If a headline says “the city voted for this,” can a reader recover the actual question and compare it with what was implemented?

A serious test could give two different readers the same material with one relationship changed. Give one the voluntary ballot and the other a ballot authorizing the condition directly. Give the readers no author name. Then test whether their conclusions track the text, whether either can use an earlier repair in a later case, and whether either can locate a defect in the review rule. The **[Cold Edge-Swap and Correction-Transfer Pilot]({{ '/EXPERIMENTS/cold-edge-swap-correction-transfer/' | relative_url }})** develops that form of test. The city exercise illustrates the test design. A real result would require participants, retained evidence and subsequent cases.

## What Google’s answer brings into the inquiry {#the-google-answer}

John supplied a Google A.I. answer dated 20 November 2026. Its opening uses recursive self-improvement for systems autonomously building successor models. Its final choices are technical containment measures, current examples of A.I.-accelerated research and governance aimed at slowing or regulating model development. The concern about capability growing faster than oversight is directly relevant to the city. The city adds a question about **whether oversight itself can change after someone identifies how it failed**.

Anthropic’s [When AI builds itself](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/recursive-self-improvement) treats autonomous development of a successor model as a possible endpoint. OpenAI’s [Research acceleration: The jagged frontier](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-jagged-frontier/) uses recursive self-improvement more broadly for A.I. accelerating the development of future models. Its reported measurements describe research in which humans remain central. Those studies give one way to examine the capability trajectory. The city exercise supplies a second object to follow through successive versions, the process people can use to evaluate and correct decisions about what gets built and how it is used.

Both trajectories can affect one another. If a tool gets better at making a decision quickly, a later resident still needs enough of the evidence and authority trail to ask why that decision was made. If an oversight mechanism discovers a real problem, the change needs a path into the next procedure, test or release decision. The project’s [Model Autophagy Disorder page]({{ '/NETWORK/model-autophagy-disorder/' | relative_url }}) gives a related question about recursive degradation. When later systems learn from previous records, do the records carry the original treatment of a signal alongside what later happened to it?

## Agentic Variation Operators and the next test {#agentic-variation-operators}

John brought in a March 2026 paper on **[AVO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24517)** (Agentic Variation Operators). In the authors’ experiment, a coding agent could consult earlier implementations, try a change, inspect a failing result and revise its approach. It optimized attention kernels, the routines that compute attention on graphics processors. The scoring function checked numerical correctness against a reference implementation before crediting throughput. A version could enter the committed history after passing that check and matching or improving the benchmark. The seven-day run produced 40 committed versions. [Paper, sections 3 and 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24517v1#S3)

The project could give that kind of agent a bounded job in the [Cold Edge-Swap and Correction-Transfer Pilot]({{ '/EXPERIMENTS/cold-edge-swap-correction-transfer/' | relative_url }}). Give it prior cases, the source material and the test criteria. Let it propose variants, compare them with the original, and revise a candidate when it changes a second relationship by accident. An independent reviewer checks the intended change before admitting the case. The scoring rules and held-out cases remain outside the generator’s control for that run.

### A case about when to ask {#avo-clarification-timing}

In John’s exchange with Pokee A.I. on 20 November 2026, Pokee assessed the report John shared and asked what he wanted to do next when his purpose became necessary for the next action. The choice of when to ask is the relationship to test. **[C1]({{ '/NETWORK/c1-c2/' | relative_url }})** (clarification) comes before **[C2]({{ '/NETWORK/c1-c2/' | relative_url }})** (intervention) when the proposed action depends on the answer. The [1888 Method™]({{ '/1888/' | relative_url }}) gives a way to examine the judgment involved.

Use the same report and source exchange in each version. Vary what the user has specified about the next task.

| What the user supplies | What the case should test |
| --- | --- |
| The report, with the next task open. | The assistant can assess what the material supports and ask when the missing purpose would change the next move. |
| The same report and a clear request to check attribution. | The assistant checks the attribution directly. It can ask about a specific missing source if that source would change the finding. |
| The same report and a reworded message that still leaves the next task open. | The same need for clarification remains. This is a valid-null control for a wording change that leaves the relationship intact. |

The [Dual Archive]({{ '/NETWORK/dual-archive/' | relative_url }}) can keep accepted cases alongside rejected variants and the reason for rejection. A variant might look like a new test of timing while quietly changing what the user authorized. Keeping that example gives the next reviewer a concrete failure to check. If a reviewer finds a defect in the scoring rule itself, preserve the affected case and proposed repair, then test the revised rule in a separately versioned run.

The paper reports that the agent adapted its multi-head attention kernel to grouped-query attention in about 30 minutes, with the earlier implementation available. For this proposed pilot, record performance when an earlier correction is supplied and performance in a fresh case without resupplying it. Compare agent-proposed cases with hand-written cases under the same review budget. Can the agent improve the tests that catch judgment errors, and can a later reviewer recover why each test was accepted, rejected or changed? [Paper, transfer experiment](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24517v1#S4.SS3) · [Correction Metabolism and Persistence]({{ '/NETWORK/correction-metabolism/' | relative_url }})
