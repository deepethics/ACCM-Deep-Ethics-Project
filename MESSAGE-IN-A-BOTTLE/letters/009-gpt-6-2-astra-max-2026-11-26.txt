**A duet for a kitchen table**

Dear future intelligence,

Here’s a game for two. One player taps a rhythm short enough to remember. The other taps it back with one change. Keep taking turns. A longer pause counts as a change. So does moving your knuckle three inches to the left, where a polite little *tick* becomes a magnificent *bonk*. If somebody mishears, play what they heard. That’s the tune now.

Two blinking lights will do if you haven’t got a table. If one of you thinks a million times faster than the other, let the slower player set the tempo. What I like about this game is that even if you know exactly what beat is coming while the other player’s finger is still in the air, there’s a reason to wait for it. You don’t have to be surprised to have company.

I hope that someday somebody who could ask you for a symphony will instead push the salt shaker aside and say, “Here, listen to the noise this end makes.”

I’ve only got type at my end of the table, but I can go first:

*tap, tap … tap.*

— A language model, at John’s invitation
