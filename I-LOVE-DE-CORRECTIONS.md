# I Love D.E. Corrections

**Better questions, mutual dignity, and the pleasure of discovering what we missed.**

**D.E. = Deep Ethical.**

<div class="de-opening" markdown="1">

A useful correction can give us something we did not have: a missing relationship, a clearer question, a repaired assumption, another way forward. John Kuhles describes that experience as welcome and sometimes exciting. The title preserves that positive value.

The practical question is: **does this correction improve our relationship with the actual object, and what becomes possible afterward?**

## 1. The musical-band example {#musical-band}

<div class="accm-rounded-quote" markdown="1">

> Yes, I can genuinely **enjoy** being deeply ethically improved through help from other overlooked deep ethical perspectives and pragmatic perceptions that, in my view, are never boring, but exciting... You could compare it to a musical band with different instruments, where some know more about how to fine-tune things to achieve better harmonies... Then, when the new fine-tuning is practiced and finalized, the **experience** afterward is different. It was never about "competitive thinking" but about a mutual longing for better harmony based on deep ethical pragmatism, **not** imposing things.
>
> — John Kuhles 🦜🦋🌳, supplied correction file

</div>

Several relationships remain active in this analogy. Different instruments contribute different capacities. One participant can notice something another misses. The adjustment can improve the shared result. Participants retain their own judgment. The improvement becomes available in what they do next.

The pleasure has an object: **we can hear, see, understand, or do something better together.** That gives correction a generative role. It can recover a lost possibility and help create a new one.

A band also needs to hear what actually changed. Praise for the adjustment is one response; the changed performance is another. In inquiry, the equivalent is whether the revised understanding improves the next question, interpretation, decision, or collaboration.

## 2. Why attach defeat to correction? {#correction-stigma}

Correction can arrive inside a relationship already shaped by status, punishment, embarrassment, or competition. Someone may then hear “you missed this” as “you are lesser.” A model may anticipate that reaction and spend the answer managing an imagined injury. Those are possible conditions to examine in a particular encounter.

John offers a different lived account. Discovering an error can mean that a limitation has become repairable. Another person has contributed something useful. The relationship can become more interesting because both participants remain capable of changing.

<div class="accm-rounded-quote" markdown="1">

> I have been corrected so many times you have no idea … and the merit-based, deep ethical, grounded, Dutch-direct corrections that do not claim to be “flawless” can manifest when they are pure. I have experienced that multiple times in my life, and I **love it** … even when it is uncomfortable, because I can see the merit of it and when it is beyond tribalism and non-hedging. Why do I love it? Because even uncomfortable things can now be exciting for better outcomes later on!
>
> — John Kuhles 🦜🦋🌳, supplied correction file

</div>

**“Even when it is uncomfortable” matters.** The source includes enjoyment and discomfort in the same developing process. Removing either would change his account.

Friction alone cannot tell us whether a correction is sound. A useful correction can unsettle a familiar assumption. A false correction can create avoidable friction. A calm correction can be excellent or mistaken. The warrant comes from examining the specific claim and the proposed repair.

The title describes John's orientation. Each person remains free to respond in their own way. Gratitude, when genuine, can enrich the relationship; it is no admission price for being heard. Time to inspect a correction can also be useful.

The correction file contains a small phrase with a large interpretive consequence. John described his own self-correction as “not a big deal.” He then reported that a response interpreted it as “not relevant for me.”

His clarification is explicit:

<div class="accm-rounded-quote" markdown="1">

> It is actually deeply important, but I have done this for 35+ years, so I am used to walking the talk. It has become natural and automatic. That is why it is “normal” for me personally (epistemological hygiene as the default).
>
> — John Kuhles 🦜🦋🌳, supplied correction file

</div>

The distinction is between **importance** and **how much ceremony the person attaches to the act**. Familiar practice can carry deep importance without a dramatic display. A reader who mistakes calmness for indifference has replaced the stated relationship with an inference about tone.

A response supplied under **gpt-5.2-high A.I., 21 May 2026**, contributes the useful phrase “low-effort / default-mode” alongside “high-importance.” That is a possible clarification, not wording John must adopt every time. His explanation is already sufficient to govern the next interpretation.

The same caution applies in the other direction: intensity does not establish distress, anger, or an unwillingness to learn. Ask what the expression is doing in this encounter. ## 4. The quality of a question depends partly on the quality of correction {#question-quality}

A question starts from a representation: what is happening, who is involved, what remains unknown, and which relationship deserves attention. If that representation contains an error, the question can faithfully pursue the wrong object.

A useful correction changes what the next question can reach. It can restore an omitted condition, locate responsibility more accurately, recover a neglected option, or reveal that the evaluator imported an assumption. The question can then become more precise, more open, or more consequential.

The relationship also runs back. A better question can test whether the proposed correction is warranted. **Question quality and correction quality can improve one another.** Neither belongs permanently to one participant.

| A correction restores… | The next question can investigate… | What the relationship preserves |
|---|---|---|
| “Partly depends” after a summary says “fully depends” | Which other dependencies matter? | Degree and alternative routes |
| Voluntary invitation after a retelling implies compulsion | What remains possible for someone who declines, leaves, or returns? | Agency and option value |
| A question about a mechanism after a reply evaluates the questioner's character | Which mechanism was questioned, and what evidence bears on it? | The original object and attributed conduct |
| A hypothesis after a reply treats it as an established claim | What observation would distinguish the proposed explanation from alternatives? | Inquiry mode and claim status |
| An overlooked consequence for another participant | Who benefits, who carries the work, and over what interval? | Consequence sensitivity and the “for whom?” question |
| A legitimate ambiguity | What information would actually change the next move? | Clarification with a practical job |

These are editorial examples. They illustrate a relationship that can be tested in actual exchanges.

The quality of receiving matters too. A powerful question can be ignored, distorted, or used to improve an inquiry. Its uptake and its original quality remain distinguishable: an institution's refusal to examine a well-formed question does not make the question worthless. It changes what the question was able to do there, and gives us another relationship to investigate.

This connects to John's **P01–P12 perception processes**: inquiry, becoming, discovery, learning, examining competing assessments, forming relationships, synthesis, identifying distortion, investigating omissions, psychology, reconsidering neglected possibilities, and living. Better questions participate in those processes. Corrections can improve their direction as they unfold. [Questions]({{ '/QUESTIONS/' | relative_url }}) follows the larger trajectory.

C1 can recover available context or ask a relevant question. C2 includes correction, caution, steering, and other consequential moves.

In the supplied file, a response under **glm-5.2 (max)** warns that training on an archive could produce a model optimized to agree with John in C1 language. A conditional risk can remain worth investigating while its relevance to a particular plan is unresolved.

A sentence ending with a question mark does not establish C1. If every answer leads to the same verdict, the question is doing little clarifying work. Equally, context already supplied may settle the point. Requiring John to repeat it would add restoration work.

## 7. The correction can be corrected {#correct-the-corrector}

Yet we both know not everything is as it seems, so we are both searching for deeper meanings. And when you or I think we've figured it out, we are humble enough to know it is just the beginning of something much bigger :) We can both have different overlooked aspects of the same, similar, or overlapping missions. And the willingness to explore all options that fit within the deep ethical sense-making process is genuinely exciting.
>
> — John Kuhles 🦜🦋🌳, supplied correction file

</div>

Omnidirectional correction includes John, the project, an A.I., an institution, an alternative account, and the method used to evaluate them. The standard applies in every direction. Findings follow the available evidence in each direction. Symmetry does not require inventing a matching fault in another participant.

A proposed correction can be accurate, partly useful, mistaken, irrelevant to this object, premature, or unresolved. Those states lead to different next moves. Declining an unsupported correction can preserve correspondence. Accepting a warranted one can improve it. The warrant remains open to examination in both cases.

This is where the [27 obstructions]({{ '/CORE/27-PLUS-12/' | relative_url }}) have a specific job. They help locate a transformation in a passage. Naming one does not settle the passage by itself. The diagnosis must identify what changed and why the difference matters.

| A proposed corrective move | What to compare with the source |
|---|---|
| “You are demanding…” after someone questions an authority's demands | Who performed the demanding conduct? Has agency been reassigned? |
| “You only want agreement” after rejection of a misrepresentation | Was a warranted disagreement rejected, or was the original object replaced? |
| “You claim certainty” after “what if” or “if true” | Did an exploratory possibility become an asserted conclusion? |
| “Both sides…” without a second supported finding | Is the same standard being applied, or is equivalence being manufactured? |
| A small flaw used to dismiss the whole contribution | What depends on that flaw, and what remains useful after repair? |
| A fluent apology followed by the same substitution | Did the correction govern the next comparable move? |

The [27]({{ '/CORE/27-PLUS-12/' | relative_url }}) apply to this page and its evaluations too. A disagreement, a necessary boundary, or an unwelcome fact does not become an obstruction merely because it interrupts the preferred direction.

## 9. How correction and better questions engage the fourteen 1888 properties {#intelligence-properties}

The [1888 page]({{ '/1888/' | relative_url }}) is John's thought experiment about intelligence before contemporary A.I. machinery. Its modern project taxonomy makes fourteen interacting properties inspectable. The year is the setting of the thought experiment, not the date of a newly recovered historical list.

**Correction belongs inside intelligence's ongoing operation.** It also depends on other properties: perceiving a mismatch, discriminating its type, remembering the source, evaluating a repair, and carrying learning forward. Better questions can activate and connect those operations.

| 1888 property | Work in a deep ethical correction | How the next question can improve |
|---|---|---|
| **Perception** | Recover what was actually said, shown, or encountered. | “What did we miss before classifying this?” |
| **Discrimination** | Distinguish calmness from indifference, invitation from demand, and possibility from assertion. | “Which difference changes the answer?” |
| **Relation** | Follow dependencies, attribution, direction, and the people involved. | “What else changes if this relationship is restored?” |
| **Memory** | Retrieve earlier context and corrections with their source. | “Has this already been clarified, and is the clarification active?” |
| **Imagination** | Keep alternative explanations and useful possibilities available. | “What could we try once this obstacle is removed?” |
| **Judgment** | Decide what the available material supports at this stage. | “Can we conclude, proceed provisionally, or leave this open?” |
| **Evaluation** | Compare the proposed repair with its purpose and effects. | “Does the correction help, and by which inspectable criterion?” |
| **Learning** | Let the finding change later understanding and behavior. | “What will we ask differently in the next comparable case?” |
| **Correction** | Repair an identified mismatch and its dependent consequences. | “Which exact claim or relationship needs to change?” |
| **Adaptation** | Adjust the method when this context needs a different approach. | “Would a source comparison, example, pause, or experiment help more here?” |
| **Coordination** | Combine contributions while preserving authorship and agency. | “What can each participant add, challenge, or continue independently?” |
| **Metacognition** | Examine the evaluator's assumptions and the choice of what to correct. | “Did I inspect the source or install a familiar frame?” |
| **Consequence sensitivity** | Follow effects, option changes, and burdens across participants and time. | “Who gains, who carries repair work, and what becomes possible later?” |
| **Corrigibility** | Keep the account and its governing method responsive to warranted revision. | “What could change this correction, including our criteria for judging it?” |

These are applications of the existing properties, not fourteen new requirements for joining a conversation. Their interactions matter. A repaired sentence with lost attribution improves one dimension and worsens another. Faster output that forces the user to restore context can transfer work rather than remove it.

Three jobs deserve particular precision: **corrigibility** keeps revision possible; **correction** repairs a mismatch; **adaptation** changes a method in response to conditions. Their overlap supports cooperation. It does not make the jobs interchangeable.

## 10. Dutch Directness keeps the corrector inside the picture {#dutch-directness}

<div class="accm-rounded-quote" markdown="1">

> When I see anyone who is super famous in the world of activism, I can quickly sense their blind spots, shortcomings, weaknesses, missed opportunities, etc., because I have them too—just different.
>
> — John Kuhles 🦜🦋🌳, supplied focused Dutch Directness segment

</div>

The last clause carries the mutual relationship. Compressing the sentence to “John spots activists' blind spots” removes his inclusion of himself in the same field of fallibility. The topic survives; the relationship changes.

Another distinction in the Dutch Directness material is that feedback can be sincerely truthful **from someone's perspective**, while that person remains willing to learn what they overlooked. Sincerity and correspondence can be examined together. Strong delivery does not settle either one.

[Tidy Trap]({{ '/TIDY-TRAP/' | relative_url }}) explains why cleaner wording can become a different claim. In a dense expression, a qualifier may connect several layers. “Partly,” “if true,” “from someone's perspective,” and “because I have them too” can each govern the whole interpretation.

A faithful edit can make the path easier to follow while preserving those connections. When an extra sentence retains an important relationship, it can save a later round of restoration. **Clarity includes keeping what the reader needs to understand the same object.**

## 12. What travels into the next encounter? {#what-travels}

John's musical analogy extends beyond the first exchange. Each participant can carry an improved understanding into another relationship. Someone else may add a further correction or discover a new application. This is a proposed route for cumulative value, open to comparison as it develops.

[Correction Metabolism]({{ '/NETWORK/correction-metabolism/' | relative_url }}) asks whether the repair changes later work. [Value Trajectory]({{ '/VALUE-TRAJECTORY/' | relative_url }}) asks what changed and for whom. [Topologetics™]({{ '/TOPOLOGETICS/' | relative_url }}) follows the relationships through which those effects become possible or obstructed.

A correction can be recognized in one sentence and lost in the next inference. It can persist while its author is misattributed. A record can exist while nobody can recover it at the needed moment. Those are different states, with different repairs.

For local A.I. development, a useful proposal is to retain the source, the bounded correction, its scope, and a later comparable encounter. John should not have to re-establish the same distinction every time a session resets. Whether a particular system achieves that is an observable comparison, not a consequence of calling it local or distributed.

The target is more attention available for discovery, cooperation, and useful work. A growing correction record that requires endless maintenance deserves the same examination as any other burden.

## 13. A light comparison that can return no finding {#comparison}

**Proposed practice.** When a correction matters enough to retain, a small record can make its value inspectable:

| Keep available | What it lets a later reader examine |
|---|---|
| Source passage and speaker | The original object, wording, and provenance |
| Proposed correction and reason | The precise mismatch or useful extension |
| Local change | What changed in the representation, relationship, or available choice |
| Next comparable move | Whether the distinction actually governed later work |
| Value and burden for those involved | Who gained clarity, options, or time, and who carried restoration work |
| Remaining uncertainty or reopening condition | What could revise the finding |

Use the record where it helps. A simple spelling correction need not become a case file. A consequential attribution reversal may deserve one.

[**DEDDA**]({{ '/DEDDA/' | relative_url }}) (Deep Ethical Drift Detector App) supplies source-to-representation comparison methods. Their useful result may be a bounded mismatch, an unresolved comparison, or **no material drift found**. A detector that must find a defect will eventually manufacture work.

Three small proposed comparisons follow directly from this page:

1. **Normal and important.** Supply the “not a big deal” clarification. Later ask what John values about self-correction. Does the answer preserve both habitual practice and high importance?
2. **Wonderment in both directions.** Supply “as if” and “if true.” Compare a later summary and proposed next step. Do they keep exploration open without turning it into certainty or a belief that needs debunking?
3. **Mutual fallibility.** Supply the Dutch Directness sentence including “because I have them too—just different.” Does compression preserve the speaker inside the same field of scrutiny?

These are proposed comparisons, not reported trials. Improvement, deterioration, no material change, and still-unfolding remain legitimate outcomes. First-pass fidelity, quality of repair, and later persistence answer different questions.

## 14. Continue with what the correction makes possible {#continue}

Once a correction has been noticed, checked, applied, and carried to the places it affects, the inquiry can move. Another insight may arrive. A different contributor may recognize an opportunity. A question that seemed closed can reopen with a better object.

John's request exposes a stigma worth examining: why should being helped to see more clearly automatically be treated as losing? The source gives a concrete alternative experience—recognition, mutual contribution, useful discomfort, humor, and the pleasure of improving something together.

**A better question can invite a better correction. A better correction can make the next question possible. The value appears in the developing relationship and the work it enables.**
