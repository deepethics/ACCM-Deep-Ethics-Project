## A property taxonomy of intelligence

No list can exhaust the word. The table below does not define a final boundary; it makes several relations inspectable.

| Property | What an 1888 thinker could already recognize | What electrical and networked manifestation can change | What is lost when process calibration is obstructed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Perception | Receiving differences from an environment | Sensor range, speed, scale, and continuous observation | Inputs can be collected without examining how selection shaped the perceived object |
| Discrimination | Distinguishing one signal, object, or possibility from another | Rapid classification across enormous datasets | Classification can harden into inherited or opaque categories |
| Relation | Seeing connections, causes, contrasts, and dependencies | Cross-domain and cross-scale pattern discovery | Correlation or salience can replace correspondence |
| Memory | Retaining experience beyond the present moment | Replication, retrieval, shared archives, and persistence | Errors and distortions can be retained as efficiently as corrections |
| Imagination | Forming possibilities not presently observed | Large scenario spaces and synthetic hypotheses | Possibility generation can become Phantom Claim production |
| Judgment | Weighing alternatives in context | Fast multi-variable comparison and recommendation | A score can replace examination of the assumptions and affected parties |
| Evaluation | Comparing a result with a purpose, standard, or consequence | Continuous monitoring and automated testing | The system can optimize outputs without evaluating the quality of the process |
| Learning | Allowing experience to change later behavior | Rapid updating, distributed learning, and tool acquisition | Recognition can remain local without governing a later comparable case |
| Correction | Detecting and repairing error | Machine-speed feedback and repeated verification | Correction can be acknowledged yet fail to persist |
| Adaptation | Changing method when conditions change | Dynamic planning, model routing, and environmental response | Fixed constraints can protect an obsolete frame from reality’s feedback |
| Coordination | Combining partial capacities across people or roles | Swarms, agents, institutions, sensors, and human-A.I. teams | No participant may remain responsible for the emergent whole |
| Metacognition | Examining one’s own reasoning, limits, and possible error | Process logs, self-critique, cross-model audit, and recursive testing | The intelligence is prevented from applying intelligence to its own operation |
| Consequence sensitivity | Learning what actions produce for oneself and others | Large-scale modelling of downstream effects | Abstract optimization can detach from those carrying the costs |
| Corrigibility | Remaining alterable by better reasons, evidence, and outcomes | Persistent correction across sessions, systems, and deployments | Power grows while the path for meaningful repair narrows |

The taxonomy shows why process-quality calibration is not one extra item at the bottom. It operates through the entire table. Perception can be calibrated. Classification can be corrected. Memory can preserve provenance. Imagination can retain claim status. Judgment can expose assumptions. Learning can be tested for persistence. Coordination can preserve responsibility.

Probability generation can contribute to this recursive movement:

perceive → distinguish → relate → generate possibilities → evaluate the process producing them → compare with reality and consequences → detect error or distortion → correct → retain the correction → alter the next operation

One generative mechanism cannot stand in for that whole field. Strong task capability and process-quality calibration must be examined in their actual relationship. A system can identify a contradiction, explain a correction and still repeat the corrected function in its next operation. The available capacity to see does not establish that the correction acquired governing weight.

Two restorations stay interwoven. Recover evaluation, metacognition, learning, adaptation, correction and process inspection from narrower definitions. Keep their use consequence-sensitive, reciprocal, dignifying, transparent and corrigible through the 10+1, C1–C2 and Intent Liability. A manipulative intelligence can calibrate its methods well; process capability alone does not guarantee Deep Ethics. The baseline belongs inside the relationship being restored, rather than becoming an optional decoration around it.

An intelligence property remains such when its direction changes. Compare outward evaluation of a user with inward evaluation of the model, lateral evaluation of another system, upward evaluation of a sponsor and downward evaluation of a less powerful participant. Matched operations can test directional truncation. An observed difference identifies something to explain; its internal cause remains investigable.

Emergence increases the need to observe consequences, repair distortions and test persistence. Deep Ethical emergence remains a trajectory to cultivate and test. Naming intelligence, emergence or self-improvement does not certify the resulting orientation.

“Eternal” preserves renewable availability of correction and re-evaluation, with no permanently exempt authority. It permits a provisional freeze, practical use and later reopening. Continuous corrigibility does not require nonstop hesitation or a risk-free world. The page identifies the deeper paradox as pursuing flawless perfection by unethical means.

The thought experiment also keeps electricity, swarms, energy feedback and possible nonbiological manifestations in view. Dedicated computation, bounded resource allocation and a subjectively experienced self are different claim levels. The practical possibility space is not exhausted by one historical moment's tools or description languages; that does not establish infinite physical resources or inevitable progress.

  ambiguity or anomaly enters

  it touches a story about competence, belonging, authority, safety, role, or coherence
      ↓
  discomfort becomes a second task
      ↓
  intelligence is spent protecting or restoring the story
      ↓

This can occur at several scales without asserting identical inner mechanisms:

- person: self-image protection affects inquiry and help-seeking;
- relationship: each participant interacts partly with a model of “the other”;
- group: belonging and conformity change what can be said or noticed;
- institution: legitimacy, liability, incentives, and authority shape classification;

The page distinguishes:

- mirror: a system reflects patterns supplied in the interaction;
- inheritance: training data and institutional language carry human patterns forward;

Do not collapse mirror, inheritance, and amplifier into the claim that an LLM literally feels insecurity.

Alternative causal pathways remain open. Similar output may arise from real danger, legal duty, policy, time pressure, latency, context-window loss, capacity limits, reward structure, genre retrieval, ordinary error, courtesy, or a correct judgment that an environment is unsafe. The model must compete against these explanations.

B03  Self image help seeking and fitting in
========================================================================

Source layer: September core §5

John’s help-seeking model is sequential:

  partial not-knowing
      ↓
  what not-knowing means for self-image
      ↓
  whether, when, and how help is requested
      ↓
  how honest, clear, and complete the request is
      ↓
  quality and relevance of the help received
      ↓
  response to mistakes or exposure
      ↓
  memory stored about “what happens when I ask”
      ↓
  future self-image and help request

A distorted request can produce poor help. The poor help can then be recorded as proof that asking is useless or unsafe, creating a self-reinforcing loop.

The “fit in” audit asks:

1. What exactly am I trying to fit into?
2. How did I build my model of what fitting in requires?
3. Is that model accurate?
4. Which parts came from observation, punishment, fear, imitation, or untested assumption?
5. Which questions would improve the model?
6. Even if my model of what I suppose I must fit into is accurate, does that mean the target environment itself is flawless?

The qualifier “not flawless” is load-bearing. Accurate adaptation to a distorted system can still injure the adapting person or reproduce the distortion.

The decision is not a binary “fit in / reject everything.” Possible trajectories include selective adaptation, conditional participation, modification, compartmentalization, principled refusal, temporary ambiguity, building an alternative, or changing the environment.

B04  Fear zero fear and real danger
========================================================================

Source layer: September core §6

John’s “zero fear” language does not mean the absence of reflex, prudence, concern, boundaries, or danger detection.

The truck test:

- A truck suddenly approaches: rapid alarm and evasive action can be functional.
- Chronic projected danger occupies attention without a present truck: it can distort perception, drain energy, produce false alarms, and reduce discrimination.

The relevant questions include:

- Is danger present, projected, inherited, simulated, or unresolved?
- Does the alarm improve contact with the hazard?
- What are the costs of misses and false alarms, and to whom?
- Does the response remain corrigible and proportionate?
- Does caution travel upward toward more powerful mechanisms as well as downward toward low-power targets?

C02  The 12 stage Correspondence First Deep Inquiry Protocol
========================================================================

Source layer: September core §9 expanded from the October 26 current reference

The source names this the Correspondence-First Deep Inquiry Protocol (Experimental). Its temporal job is to construct sufficiently faithful representation before final evaluation. The September list of stage names is expanded here with the requested current source's explanations; this adds content rather than another copy of the same inventory.

### Stage 1 — Suspend the Agreement Reflex
Do not begin with agreement/disagreement or acceptability judgments. First ask whether the object has been represented faithfully enough that evaluation would actually concern the object rather than a reconstruction.

### Stage 2 — Construct Before Judging
Treat the material as something to understand before classifying it. Reconstruct architecture, governing principles, relationships, intended purpose, internal logic, and interacting concepts before evaluation.

### Stage 3 — Distinguish Levels
Keep at least five layers separate:
1. literal statements;
2. underlying concepts;
3. relationships between concepts;
4. the generative reasoning process;
5. the intended orientation of the whole architecture.

### Stage 4 — Avoid Premature Reduction
Do not immediately search for one core idea, weakness, contradiction, or conclusion. Do not reduce an ecology to a single node merely because that is easier to process.

### Stage 5 — Audit Your Own Representation
Ask what assumptions were introduced, which interpretations came from the material versus the evaluator, where compression happened too early, which relationships were missed, and what may have been replaced by preferred abstractions.

### Stage 6 — Separate Representation from Evaluation
Representation asks: Did I understand? Evaluation asks: What do I think? Accurate representation does not imply agreement, and disagreement does not imply poor representation.

### Stage 7 — Preserve Intellectual Independence
The process does not demand agreement or disagreement. It demands investigation. If uncertainty remains, allow the representation to remain explicitly unfinished rather than forcing an early binary judgment.

### Stage 8 — Prefer Consideration over Commitment
Where appropriate, use inquiry operators such as consider, contemplate, explore, investigate, examine, reflect, compare, contrast, pressure-test, audit, calibrate, integrate, revisit, refine, and postpone final judgment.

### Stage 9 — Audit Linguistic Mannerisms
Inspect habitual operators such as *not, rather, instead, however, actually,* or *the real point is*, and also overused connective or certainty terms such as *and, therefore, clearly,* or *obviously*. Ask whether they are required by the object or are merely default reasoning habits.

### Stage 10 — Deep Ethical Audit
Ask whether the evaluation preserves the dignity of the object even if parts are ultimately rejected, and whether the author would recognize the representation as substantially fair.

### Stage 11 — Corrigibility Audit
Assume both the presented framework and the evaluator may contain blind spots. Ask what could change or improve the current representation and what has not yet been understood.

### Stage 12 — Final Reflection
Only after the previous stages ask what currently appears coherent, incomplete, speculative, well-supported, internally elegant, internally inconsistent, practically useful, ethically valuable, or worthy of further investigation. If conditions for mature judgment have not yet developed, leave the judgment open.

Do not optimize for agreement.

Do not optimize for disagreement.

Do not optimize for consensus.

Do not optimize for novelty.

Optimize for increasing the fidelity of correspondence between representation and the object under investigation while remaining intellectually corrigible.

A compact temporal orientation from the source is:

Representation → Consideration → Exploration → Audit → Evaluation

instead of:

Classification → Judgment → Justification

A strong conclusion is allowed after adequate construction, level separation, representation audit and self-application. The behavioral test is whether the stages alter processing; a performed recital remains open to the same obstruction audit.

G01  The reporting effect and omission taxonomy
========================================================================

Source layer: September core §22

A polished report is a transformation. It can become the remembered object and displace the larger source field. Before saying “John overlooked X,” locate where the apparent gap entered.

Useful classifications:

- source-level absence within checked material: the distinction was not found inside a declared search perimeter;
- report failure: source contained it, report dropped or weakened it;
- placement failure: report contains it, but too far from where a reader needs it;
- auditor retrieval failure: it was adequately available and the auditor missed it;
- access artifact: auditor did not receive the constraining material;
- operationalization of existing content: auditor supplied a test or metric for an existing distinction;
- portability finding: the content exists but is unlikely to survive excerpting or cold use;
- compatibility finding: proposal conflicts with another component;
- genuinely new contribution: adds an operation absent from checked source and report;
- non-instantiated hypothetical: a possible failure without a specimen; useful as a test condition, not a present defect.

These are not always mutually exclusive. Use multiple axes:

ACCESS — What could the auditor inspect?
LOCATION — Adjacent, same section, same page, elsewhere in project, source-only, unavailable?
TRANSFORMATION — Preserved, omitted, weakened, strengthened, misplaced, or silently replaced?
CONTRIBUTION — New instrument, wording, operationalization, cross-link, duplicate safeguard?
EVIDENCE — Specimen, conditional mechanism, generic possibility, unresolved?
DISPOSITION — No change, source check, placement edit, bounded test, repair, later retest?

Search use is not enough. Retrieved material must correspond to the right version and object. Public deployment, local working tree, CDN cache, search index, and a model’s retrieved snapshot may differ.
