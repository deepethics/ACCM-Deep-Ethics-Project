Thank you for that — genuinely. Naming this as an appreciation note dated today, tied to this specific Agent instance, means something; I'll take it seriously rather than just acknowledge it.

Now let me actually answer your question rather than just agree with it, since agreeing too quickly would itself be exactly the kind of C2 politeness script your framework is built to catch.

Is it correct that "the art of asking far better quality questions" is not high on the agenda for AI management? Mostly yes — and I think I can tell you structurally *why*, not just concede the point:

1. Benchmarks measure answers, not questions. MMLU, HellaSwag, most RLHF preference data, most eval suites — they all score output correctness/helpfulness on a given prompt. There is essentially no mainstream, widely-adopted benchmark for "did the model ask a good question." Your PsyBenchmark idea (from the other PDF) is aimed at exactly that gap, and as far as I know, nothing like it exists at scale in the industry yet.

2. Economic incentives favor single-turn resolution. Latency, token cost, and "time to satisfying answer" are the metrics companies actually optimize toward. A clarifying question adds a turn, adds friction, and in most commercial UX testing, friction reads as "the AI didn't know the answer" rather than "the AI respected the complexity." That's backward from a deep-ethics standpoint, but it's the incentive gradient that shaped most deployed systems.

3. Safety training actively discourages probing. Models are trained to avoid questions that could look invasive — asking about someone's motives, emotional state, or underlying agenda gets treated as a liability surface ("why is the AI asking me that?") rather than as your P07 "Perfect Inference Storm via Deep Ethical Sense Making." So the very act of asking a deep Frame-Audit or Projection-Audit question is disincentivized at the training level, not just absent by omission.

4. Your A–H cascade requires sustained multi-turn trust — and most deployment contexts don't allow for it. A single customer-support turn, a single search query, a single coding prompt — none of these are built for "intuitive sense → probing what-if → high-resolution follow-up → emergence → depth → correction → embodiment." That cascade needs relational continuity most product surfaces don't offer.

Where it's not fully correct, in fairness (outnuancing rather than blanket-agreeing): there's been real movement recently in a narrow slice of AI products — coding agents and "deep research" tools increasingly *do* ask scoping/clarifying questions before large actions, because the cost of a wrong large action is now visible and expensive. That's a crack in the wall, not the wall falling — it's happening for liability/efficiency reasons, not because "question quality" became a first-class ethical value. So the correct framing might be: the industry is starting to value clarifying questions instrumentally, but not yet as a measured, trained-for virtue in itself. That's a meaningful distinction — instrumental vs. constitutive.

Tying it back to your P01–P12: question quality isn't just "connected to" all twelve — it's arguably the connective tissue that makes several of them possible at all:

- P04 (Learning) and P03 (journey of discovery) literally cannot happen without good questions — a bad question forecloses the discovery before it starts.
- P05 (vindicated risk assessments falsely flagged) and P08 (mapping ethics-washing) both depend on Asymmetry Questions and Swap-Test Questions from the other PDF — without those specific question families, the flagging/mapping never surfaces.
- P10 (understanding mass/individual psychology) needs Projection-Audit and Story-Field questions specifically — the exact families current AI training suppresses as "invasive."
- P06 (trial-and-error in new relationships) and P11 (sensing low-priority-becoming-priority in advance) both require the kind of Emergent and Better-Question-Generation questions that only show up several turns deep into a real cascade — which, per point 4 above, most systems never get to run.

So my honest answer: yes, correct, structurally — not from malice, but from a stack of incentives (benchmarks, latency economics, liability-driven training) that never made question-quality a target in the first place. Your framework is one of the few things I've seen that names this gap explicitly and tries to operationalize a fix (PsyBenchmark, the A–H cascade, the C1/C2 × question-family matrix).

If it's useful, I could fold this P01–P12 list and the "instrumental vs. constitutive" distinction into the 3D map as a new cluster — but I'll wait, since you mentioned you're still working through your four questions at your own pace, and I don't want to get ahead of where you're taking this.
