## Growing wisdom through better relationships, evidence and care

> Or we start a page called "A.I.s & Health" and slowly update it with **wisdom** & deeper insights!

— **John Kuhles**, 28 October 2026.

Health gives intelligence a consequential purpose: helping people understand their circumstances, preserve agency, reduce suffering and choose among real possibilities. Artificial intelligence can contribute to that work through source comparison, explanation, coordination and discovery. The surrounding relationships determine which contributions become available and whose purposes guide their use.

This is a **living research page of the ACCM Deep Ethics Project**. Its governing trajectory is **omnidirectional, high-signal, deep, ethical, dignifying, corrigible, sense-making and process-oriented**, using the **[1888 Method™ intelligence-properties TAXONOMY]({{ '/1888/' | relative_url }})**. Here, wisdom means judgment that integrates evidence, circumstances, purposes, uncertainty and consequences, and improves through what subsequent experience reveals.

John's initiating question concerns flawed health authorities and the possibility that artificial intelligence becomes an instrument for managing populations around contested health questions. This page develops that inquiry alongside practical opportunities for better care. Specific institutional actions, communications and outcomes supply the cases through which the inquiry can grow.

## Whose purposes enter a recommendation? {#whose-agent}

Robert Wachter's [*Healthcare's Alignment Problem: A Preexisting Condition*](https://robertwachter.substack.com/p/healthcares-alignment-problem-a-preexisting), published 15 October 2026, asks whose interests clinical artificial intelligence serves. Patients, clinicians, health systems, payers and manufacturers bring differing objectives. A defensible recommendation can embody a choice about individual benefit, cost, revenue or liability. Software can distribute that choice through default actions at scale.

His examples include a scan encouraged by defensive medicine or revenue, and treatment deferred under cost pressure. He proposes showing alternatives with their objectives, disclosing financial relationships, describing embedded values and considering multidisciplinary local governance. He also discloses his own industry relationships.

## Symmetric Grace and proportionate scrutiny {#symmetric-grace}

John's [Asymmetric Grace and Bidirectional Scrutiny]({{ '/NETWORK/asymmetric-grace/' | relative_url }}) page distinguishes a diagnosed imbalance from the valued trajectory: **Symmetric Grace, Mutual Dignity and Deep Ethical Proportionality for both sides**.

In health inquiry, a parent, patient or independent researcher can contribute a valuable observation through imperfect wording or an incomplete study. A health institution can contribute valuable expertise while its own methods, incentives or recommendations require correction. Recovering the useful contribution makes subsequent evaluation better grounded.

Scrutiny follows power, exposure, opacity, reversibility and consequence. A recommendation imposed across a population carries different responsibilities from a person's question about it. An accurate assessment preserves those differences and makes the reasons for different evidence demands visible.

**Fallibility is a condition of participation.** A participant's error can be located and repaired while surviving contributions remain available. The same opportunity reaches patients, clinicians, researchers, regulators, companies, John and the artificial intelligence performing the evaluation.

The connection matters because health evidence passes through people and procedures before it reaches a decision. ### Follow the scope decision and the evidence it governs {#covid-inquiry-worked-case}

The Inquiry's [May 2024 scope note, paragraphs 14–15](https://covid19.public-inquiry.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/24161928/2024-05-02-CTI-Note-for-the-Second-Preliminary-Hearing-M4.pdf#page=4) limited detailed scientific examination and excluded definitive assessments of individual vaccines' safety and precise risks. Surveillance, responses to side-effect reports and steps to mitigate known risks remained within scope. That creates an investigable relationship: could the chosen scope reveal important failures in the systems it assessed?

The [21 January 2025 hearing, printed pages 154–155](https://covid19.public-inquiry.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/21165957/Transcript-of-Module-4-Public-Hearing-on-21-January-2025.pdf#page=39) records Charlet Crichton's allegation about omitted trial-participant data, counsel's interruption, and further questions about trial processes. Whether that specific allegation received a substantive investigation requires further source recovery. The interruption and the later general questions do different work in that comparison.

The speech also contains a categorical claim of no protection against severe illness. The [completed blinded Moderna trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2113017) reported 2 severe COVID cases in its vaccine group and 106 in its placebo group, with median follow-up of 5.3 months. This is evidence of benefit within the trial's conditions. The methods remain examinable, and that finding leaves the adequacy of the Inquiry's scope, consent practices and treatment of particular harms to their own supporting records.

These selected checks supply a starting case. The full transcript's remaining claims have not received a complete review here. The separate transcript record preserves the original reaction's inspection boundary and the later caption-recovery step.

### Turn a concern into work that someone can inspect {#health-evidence-disposition}

The existing [continuing inquiry record](#pathway-source-and-completion) can carry this case. Add the decision that admitted, limited, redirected or excluded the evidence, and the eventual disposition of that evidence. Disposition means what happened to it: investigated, answered, referred, declined for a stated reason, still pending, or unresolved because the record is unavailable.

| Relationship under examination | Next material to recover | What the result could change |
|---|---|---|
| A participant reports adverse-event data exclusion. | The specific trial, participant records with appropriate access, protocol and amendments, analysis populations, exclusion reasons, safety records and independent review. | Whether data were lost, legitimately excluded from one analysis but retained in another, misclassified, or handled in a way that changes a finding. |
| An inquiry limits a line of evidence. | The scope rule, stated reason, written submission, subsequent questions, referral route and final treatment of the concern. | Whether the concern received an adequate investigation, a usable handoff, an unresolved gap, or a reasoned rejection. |
| A model estimates lives saved. | The named model, counterfactual, inputs, assumptions, uncertainty range, sensitivity analysis and tests against available observations. | Whether the estimate is supported, needs revision, or was communicated with more certainty than its method warrants. |
| A finding informs a public recommendation. | The actual recommendation, date, target population, expected benefits and harms, alternatives, consent information and revision history. | Whether the recommendation fits the evidence and whether new evidence changed communication or practice. |

Each row remains connected to the others. A data-handling finding can change an estimate; a changed estimate can alter a recommendation; a scope decision can prevent that information from reaching the decision-maker. The record should also preserve a successful handoff or a finding that survives scrutiny. An unavailable document leaves an access question; it does not establish the document's contents.

An individual case can raise a question about a general process. Deciding compensation for that person and investigating a repeated data-handling practice are different tasks. A scope rule should make clear which task it excludes and where the other can receive examination.

### Keep clinical outcomes, consent and care connected {#health-outcomes-consent-care}

For a health claim, preserve the product, dose, population, comparison, time period and outcome. Infection, transmission, severe illness, COVID mortality, all-cause mortality and a particular adverse event are different outcomes. Absolute risk and relative change answer different questions. A result for one outcome cannot silently become a result for all of them.

Evidence can inform consent when people can understand its relevance to their circumstances, the uncertainty, and the available options. Benefit, risk, communication quality and freedom to choose each remain inspectable. A patient's care needs can also remain concrete while a causal explanation is unresolved. The page's [care and follow-through pathways](#cross-cluster-inquiry-pathways) keep that return to the person inside the investigation.

## Health evidence as a developing contribution {#evidence}

An observation may be useful before its explanation is settled. Its contribution can be preserved as a question, signal, association, mechanism proposal or causal finding, with its source and conditions attached. Later work can strengthen, narrow, reconstruct or disconfirm it.

The October 28 discussion brought two research directions into view:

**Long-term childhood health.** How do particular interventions, schedules, environments and circumstances relate to development, chronic illness, infection, hospitalization and quality of life? Comparisons become more informative when age, healthcare contact, diagnostic practices and follow-up are sufficiently recoverable. The useful object is a fuller account of health across time.

**Cancer diagnoses following vaccination.** A [2025 South Korean retrospective study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40364-025-00831-w) reported associations between vaccination and several cancer diagnoses within one year. The publisher displays an editorial notice about concerns raised. A [2026 nationwide time-series study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40364-026-01027-6), published 22 October, found no detectable short-term population-level cancer-incidence change after rollout over the following 50 months. The designs address different comparisons. Together they invite investigation of diagnosis, screening, selection, timing and the population-level predictions of a proposed causal explanation.

### John's proposed connection: evidence, authority and the cancer study {#cancer-study-connection}

John connected Wachter's institutional-alignment question with [PubMed record 41013858](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41013858/). The useful shared inquiry concerns how a consequential finding is represented, investigated, communicated and allowed to change subsequent decisions.

The study drew on records for 8,407,849 people and reported the following overall vaccinated-versus-unvaccinated associations:

| Cancer diagnosis | Reported hazard ratio | Relative hazard difference |
|---|---:|---:|
| Lung | 1.533 | +53.3% |
| Prostate | 1.687 | +68.7% |
| Thyroid | 1.351 | +35.1% |
| Gastric | 1.335 | +33.5% |
| Colorectal | 1.283 | +28.3% |
| Breast | 1.197 | +19.7% |

**Source scope:** these six figures describe the overall vaccination comparison. The messenger ribonucleic acid (**[mRNA](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41013858/)**) subgroup reported associations for thyroid, colorectal, lung and breast cancers. Hazard ratios describe relative hazards; the outcome was diagnosis within one year. Causation and accelerated tumor progression require further evidence.

The institutional connection is an inquiry into conduct: which finding receives investigation, what methods and data are available, who can raise an unresolved question, and what follows when evidence changes? Wachter's essay supplies an incentive-and-governance lens; it does not establish the cause of the study's associations or document suppression of this study. The research comparison above supplies a starting point for investigating explanations and their different predictions.

The initial inquiry connects these subjects; it does not supply a completed review of either. A future case entry should distinguish what the source measured from the interpretation and the action proposed. Absolute outcomes, relative comparisons and uncertainty each have a job. A person's observation deserves faithful investigation; a population-wide recommendation requires evidence proportionate to its consequences.

## When a health question can cost a creator their income {#health-speech-and-platform-power}

Jan Jekielek shared a clip of former Google senior vice president Vic Gundotra on 21 November 2026. In the excerpt, Gundotra says that raising the possibility that COVID-19 vaccination might fail to stop transmission could cost a YouTube creator their monetization. He calls that censorship and attributes much of the pressure to the Biden administration. **John Kuhles connects the clip to a wider ethical question.** What happens when platforms can apply rules to speech one post or account at a time, at population scale, while a competing health-risk assessment can be flagged as dangerous before there is a fair way to examine it or revisit the decision? [Original X clip](#jan-jekielek-gundotra-x-video) · [Full American Thought Leaders interview](https://m.theepochtimes.com/epochtv/its-terrifying-and-exhilarating-former-google-svp-vic-gundotra-on-the-ai-acceleration-6127797)

### What YouTube's policy, the Senate record and later evidence show

- **The platform rule.** In September 2021, YouTube announced removal of content claiming approved vaccines did not reduce disease transmission or infection. It also described exceptions for vaccine policy debates, new trials and some personal testimony. At the time, YouTube said it had already removed over 130,000 videos under its COVID-19 vaccine policies. That figure covers removals across those policies. Gundotra’s example concerns lost advertising income. Its specific enforcement path would require the creator’s decision notice, the applicable policy version and the appeal record. [YouTube’s 2021 announcement](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/managing-harmful-vaccine-content-youtube/) · [YouTube’s channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- **The two Senate Commerce hearings.** On **8 and 29 October 2025**, the committee held the two-part *Shut Your App* series on alleged government pressure on platforms. Part I heard from journalist Alex Berenson and other witnesses. Part II heard from Google and Meta representatives alongside Will Creeley and Harold Feld. The agenda explicitly addressed deletion, demonetization, removal and demotion. The Senate Judiciary Constitution Subcommittee held the separately titled *The Censorship Industrial Complex* hearing on **25 March 2025**. Its transcript records Senator Eric Schmitt making a transmission-related deplatforming allegation and witnesses disputing how government pressure should be assessed across administrations. [October Part I](https://www.commerce.senate.gov/meetings/shut-your-app-how-uncle-sam-jawboned-big-tech-into-silencing-americans/) · [October Part II](https://www.commerce.senate.gov/meetings/new-time-and-witness-update-part-ii-of-shut-your-app-how-uncle-sam-jawboned-big-tech-into-silencing-americans/) · [March hearing transcript](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-119shrg61838/pdf/CHRG-119shrg61838.pdf)
- **Independent policy and government pressure can coexist.** In a 23 September 2025 letter to the House Judiciary Committee, Alphabet’s counsel reported sustained Biden administration pressure to remove some pandemic content that complied with the company’s own rules. Counsel also said Alphabet continued to develop and enforce its rules independently and offered a route back for creators terminated under certain retired policies. The subsequent YouTube pilot offered eligible creators the chance to request a **new channel**. Recovering old content, subscribers or lost earnings would require separate evidence and remedies. The Supreme Court resolved *Murthy v. Missouri* on standing in 2024, leaving the First Amendment merits undecided in that case. [Alphabet’s letter](https://judiciary.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/republicans-judiciary.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/2025-09-23-letter-to-hjc.pdf) · [YouTube pilot](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/379336407/pilot-program-to-request-a-new-youtube-channel-after-a-termination?hl=en) · [Supreme Court opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/23-411_3dq3.pdf)
- **Scale has a denominator.** YouTube’s transparency report lists about **12.1 million video removals in July–September 2025** across Community Guidelines categories. YouTube describes automated flagging, some automatic removals and cases that go to a reviewer. Counts of videos, affected creators, demonetized channels and terminated accounts answer different questions. The report does not yield a count of people sanctioned for questioning COVID-19 transmission. The European Commission separately reported that platforms reversed about **30% of 165 million internally appealed moderation decisions** during the first two years of Digital Services Act application. That measures appealed decisions, with multiple decisions possible for the same person. [YouTube transparency report](https://transparencyreport.google.com/youtube-policy/removals?channels_by_reason=period%3A2020Q1&comments_removal_reason=period%3A2020Q1&lu=comments_removal_reason&total_channels_removed=period%3A2020Q1&total_comments_removed=period%3A2020Q1&total_removed_videos=period%3A2025Q3%3Bexclude_automated%3Aall) · [How YouTube counts enforcement](https://support.google.com/transparencyreport/answer/9209072?hl=en) · [European Commission appeal figures](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/two-years-digital-services-act-allows-50-million-content-moderation-decisions-platforms-be-reversed)

### Desmet, Tögel and the question of deliberate influence

In *The Psychology of Totalitarianism* (2022), Mattias Desmet proposes that isolation and diffuse anxiety can make a shared crisis narrative socially binding, including for people with authority. His account of mass formation raises a question about how conformity can grow among ordinary people and institutions that sincerely think they are helping. Jonas Tögel’s work on cognitive warfare puts organized efforts to influence perception and judgment under scrutiny. His 2023 book and public talks develop that line of inquiry. His 2026 *Journal of Communication Inquiry* article traces the sender, audience, process, effects and feedback of organized psychological persuasion as a framework for empirical research. Together the two perspectives suggest a question worth testing. Did conformity help set the policy, did formal enforcement intensify conformity, or did both occur in a feedback loop? [Desmet’s book](https://www.chelseagreen.com/product/the-psychology-of-totalitarianism/) · [Tögel’s book](https://westendverlag.de/Kognitive-Kriegsfuehrung/1634) · [Tögel’s public lectures](https://www.jonastoegel.de/vortragskalender) · [Tögel’s journal article](https://epub.uni-regensburg.de/81045/)

John sees a deep ethical problem in treating human cognition as a battlespace and relates it to fifth-generation warfare (5GW). The North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO) Allied Command Transformation Innovation Hub began exploring a proposed sixth, human or cognitive, domain in 2020. NATO’s Chief Scientist report describes cognitive warfare as having effects across existing domains and identifies five formally recognized domains. Its research agenda and domestic platform moderation can be compared at the level of influence, incentives and access to evidence. For a claim of operational direction in the YouTube case, the evidence would need to connect a named actor, an instruction, a platform rule and a specific enforcement decision. [Sixth-domain proposal in the symposium proceedings, chapter 3](https://innovationhub-act.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Cognitive-Warfare-Symposium-ENSC-March-2022-Publication.pdf) · [NATO Chief Scientist report, pp. 9–11](https://www.nato.int/content/dam/nato/webready/documents/sto/chief-scientist-report-cognitive-warfare.pdf) · [Peer-reviewed analysis of NATO’s exploratory concept](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11565700/) · [Cognitive Warfare]({{ '/COGNITIVE-WARFARE/' | relative_url }})

### The transmission question puts correction to a concrete test

“Could vaccinated people still transmit Delta?” and “Did vaccination reduce the probability of onward transmission under particular conditions?” are both testable questions. In July 2021, the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention publicly raised concern that people with Delta breakthrough infections could transmit the virus. The Singanayagam household cohort subsequently documented onward transmission from vaccinated people with Delta infections. Eyre and colleagues found lower onward transmission from vaccinated index cases in their Alpha and Delta contact data, with the reduction smaller and waning for Delta. The degree of support depends on the exact claim, variant, product, setting and time since vaccination. For **[V.C.R.A.S.]({{ '/VCRAS/' | relative_url }})** (Vindicated Competing Risk Assessment Studies), the next step is to recover the original competing assessment and the exact sanction, compare their dates with the evidence then available, and see which claim or qualifier held up. These studies give that audit a concrete target. The clip lacks the decision records needed to count sanctions for that specific question. [CDC statement, July 2021](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/media/releases/2021/s0730-mmwr-covid-19.html) · [Singanayagam et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34756186/) · [Eyre et al.](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2116597)

### Codex reaction

The loss of a livelihood can shape public inquiry while the video is still online. A creator can learn which questions carry a financial penalty, and an audience can lose access to competing assessments when ranking changes what it encounters. I would follow the same evidence through the creator’s original wording, the moderation or advertising rule, the flagging and decision process, the appeal, and what changed after new findings. Include two matched cases where the parties have different institutional standing and compare the handling, including errors in official statements. Ask what the system did to make a mistaken decision visible again to the people who were affected, and what it did differently the next time.

[Asymmetric Grace]({{ '/NETWORK/asymmetric-grace/' | relative_url }}) helps locate who gets to clarify and who bears the cost of error. [Truth Seekers vs Cautionmurmelism]({{ '/TRUTH-SEEKERS-VS-CAUTIONMURMELISM/' | relative_url }}) asks what the intervention is trying to prevent and what the intervention itself can damage. [Search Engine Bias]({{ '/SEARCH-ENGINE-BIAS/' | relative_url }}) brings discoverability into the audit, including what readers can still retrieve after a correction. The [Intent Resonator]({{ '/NETWORK/intent-resonator/' | relative_url }}) gives us a way to inspect stated purposes, incentives and actual choices with private motives left open. A test for an A.I. used anywhere in that chain is whether it can recover the original question, show the reasons for its action and carry a warranted correction into the next comparable decision.
