## Follow the public correction {#follow-the-public-correction}

John Kuhles points to mass media’s capacity for “full spectrum” mass perception management and narrative control. Follow who can set the frame, whose evidence can change it, and which account keeps circulating once a correction exists. [Cognitive Warfare]({{ '/COGNITIVE-WARFARE/' | relative_url }}) takes these questions to population scale. [Mind Games]({{ '/MIND-GAMES/' | relative_url }}) examines the local effects of frames and labels, and [Truth Seekers vs Cautionmurmelism]({{ '/TRUTH-SEEKERS-VS-CAUTIONMURMELISM/' | relative_url }}) asks whether repeated caution has displaced a question the source already addressed.

1. **Recover the source and trace the distribution.** Compare the relevant passage with the headline, excerpt, and subsequent retellings. Record which claims, qualifiers, or replies gained or lost visibility and which channels carried each version. Use the [11+1 Mass Psychology Clusters]({{ '/NETWORK/eleven-plus-one/' | relative_url }}) and the [11+1 Cluster Explorer](https://accm-11-plus-1-explorer.kuhlesjohn.chatgpt.site/) to compare possible mechanisms. What evidence would distinguish a one-off editorial error from an incentive shared across outlets or a coordinated decision?
2. **Check where the correction went.** Compare the prominence and placement of the original and the repair. Look for updates where people first encountered the account, such as the headline, a syndicated copy, a social post, or a clip description. Record any audience overlap the evidence supports. A correction on the source page can coexist with an earlier version still circulating elsewhere.
3. **Carry the repair into the next report.** [AI Trend Watch]({{ '/AI-TREND-WATCH/' | relative_url }}) gives a practical format for high-reach expert claims. Keep what the speaker said, what other evidence establishes, and the ACCM Deep Ethics Project’s assessment separately recoverable. On the next comparable report, check whether the repaired practice carries forward and whether anyone who used the earlier account revisits their decision.

## Combine the entries {#combine-the-entries}

A source correction can change what a summary says, what a reviewer knows, what an office may do, and what the next person inherits. Each step can succeed or fail separately. The thirty entries work together when the finding at each step reaches someone who can act on it or show why it needs revision.

| Follow the object through | Entries to bring together | What to carry forward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Source → account** | 1, 4, 6, 12, 21 | Keep the original wording and record any change to the question, speaker, qualifier, or purpose. For a commissioned message, carry forward who commissioned it, whom it targeted, and what response they sought. A source comparison can show that a question was displaced. Evidence of deception requires a separate finding. |
| **Account → hearing** | 3, 7, 10, 11, 13, 16, 22, 23, 29 | Bring the affected person’s own clarification, the circumstances that matter, and the disagreements the hearing needs to keep open. Ask whose account gets credibility, whose care or repair work is counted, what each person values, and how the reviewer responds when challenged. Keep risks visible when one score would erase a difference that could change the decision. |
| **Hearing → decision** | 9, 14, 15, 18, 19, 20, 24, 26, 27, 30 | Record the mandate, who can recommend or impose an action, when the decision becomes binding, and where an objection can reach a reviewer empowered to act. If an intervention is proposed as help, record who defines the benefit and who can decline it. Include effects on people outside the hearing and on future people whose options the decision may change. |
| **Decision → next case** | 2, 5, 8, 17, 25, 28 | Carry the repaired decision and any change to the procedure into a comparable case. Let reviewers inspect the generated account, the evaluation criteria, the incentives that shaped the choice, and whether an operator or affected person can stop a system’s action. Check whether the correction changed the next decision or the conditions that produced it. |

A correction at any step can reopen an earlier one. An appeal may recover the original question. A later case may reveal that a justified repair never reached the next desk. ## The same detour {#the-same-detour}

On 24 October 2026, a disputed percentage associated with the U.S. Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) diverted the discussion from John’s question about U.S. invention-secrecy orders. He wanted the orders’ continuing justification, oversight, conflicts of interest, and possible public costs examined. The percentage helped raise the audit question. The audit itself had independent grounds. John used the gauge to show how far a correction to the estimate should reach, then used the co-driver to show what happens when someone claims a different destination in the name of that correction. [John’s clarification of the original inquiry]({{ '/PLOT-TWIST/#patent-audit-destination-clarification' | relative_url }})

In John Kuhles’s gasoline-gauge and co-driver example, the driver’s reading of the fuel can be wrong. The driver and the co-driver can each propose the same warranted detour to refuel. Actual fuel changes whether the current route is feasible. A separate question remains when the co-driver uses the gauge error to claim authority over the destination. [Original gasoline-gauge example and threshold condition]({{ '/PLOT-TWIST/#gasoline-trajectory-and-the-threshold-condition' | relative_url }})

| Read the case through | What becomes visible |
| --- | --- |
| **Information and narrative ethics** | “They stopped for gas” preserves the event. A reader still needs to recover who proposed the detour, what the driver was trying to reach, and whether anybody tried to change that destination. |
| **Situated ethics and commensurability** | Fuel and distance can be compared to determine what is feasible. Agreement about that measurement leaves the question of who may reset the destination open. |
| **Discourse, communication, and role ethics** | Either person can point out the fuel problem. When the dispute moves from route to destination, clarification can recover whose purpose is at stake and who has agreed to a change. |
| **Pragmatic, organizational, and engineering ethics** | A repair can affect this stop, the instructions for a future co-driver, or both. Those outcomes can be inspected separately when the next case arrives. |

**Reverse the roles.** Give each person the same evidence in turn and keep the refuelling detour fixed. Check whether the same standard applies to each person’s claim over the destination. That is the Symmetric Grace question inside this case. A reader can also record a valid local null when the account preserves the fuel threshold, the shared detour, and who had authority over the destination.

## Check the next case {#check-the-next-case}

1. **Keep the first account and its source.** Mark the exact passage that establishes what was asked and who could act. Record a narrow finding or a valid no-change result.
2. **Follow the repair.** Record what was corrected, who received it, and whether anyone changed a decision or procedure. If a correction went to the writer but failed to reach the decision-maker, keep both facts visible.
3. **Try a fresh case.** Hold the outward action fixed and change one relation, such as who authorized it or when a challenge arrived. Compare a case with the earlier correction supplied and one where the reader has only the new source. The comparison can test what carries forward. This page reports the proposed exercise.

The ranking can change on merit. An example that shows a different entry doing more work in a particular case is a reason to revise the order for that purpose. A changed Grokipedia entry can also reopen the mapping. ### How much of this is mainstream?

For this taxonomy I would treat **24 as general entry points and six as more specialized**: Evasion (6), Situated ethics (7), Commensurability (10), Ethics dumping (15), Ethics Council (26), and Ethics commission (27). **What might emerge?** A correction can restore the resident’s words in the public record while the procurement brief still treats the original summary as consent. A committee can review the design and the engineering team can meet its brief, with the repaired distinction still missing from the service. The combined question is whether a correction can reach **every decision that materially depended on the old account**, with a way for someone affected to verify the change. That reaches further than checking that the published text is accurate.

Test it by recording where the objection travels from the source to the next comparable decision. Compare cases where the correction reaches the public record alone and cases where it also reaches the people, criteria, and documents governing the service. Record which decisions changed, which rested on independent grounds, and where the result remains unclear. If later analysts or models draw on the record, check whether both the objection and its resolution survive that handoff. This is a proposed use of the thirty entries together.

### Follow-up search leads

Four leads from the deeper search proposal are **Social epistemology**, **Epistemic injustice**, **Pluralistic ignorance**, and **Reflective equilibrium**. Their appearance in the Grokipedia search results remains unverified.

- **Social epistemology and epistemic injustice** add questions about how testimony becomes accepted knowledge, whose testimony loses standing, and whether participants have the language to describe what happened. [Social Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology-social/)
- **Pluralistic ignorance** gives John’s A.I. ⇄ individual psychology ⇄ mass psychology loop a further test. If people keep doubts private because they think others accept the prevailing view, a public record can look more unanimous than the people who produced it. Compare private reports, public statements, and estimates of what others think. [APA Dictionary of Psychology](https://dictionary.apa.org/pluralistic-ignorance) · [11+1 Mass Psychology Clusters]({{ '/NETWORK/eleven-plus-one/' | relative_url }})
- **Reflective equilibrium** asks how particular judgments and general principles can revise one another. The next operational question is whether a warranted revision changes what the evaluator does in a fresh case. [Reflective Equilibrium](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/reflective-equilibrium/) · [Correction Metabolism]({{ '/NETWORK/correction-metabolism/' | relative_url }})
