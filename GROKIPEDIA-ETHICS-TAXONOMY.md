## Combine the entries {#combine-the-entries}

A source correction can change what a summary says, what a reviewer knows, what an office may do, and what the next person inherits. Each step can succeed or fail separately. The untapped potential is in carrying what one inquiry finds into the next, where somebody can act on it or show why it needs revision.

| Follow the object through | Entries to bring together | What to carry forward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Source → account** | 1, 4, 6, 12 | The original wording and a record of any change to the question, speaker, qualifier, or purpose. The evasion entry concerns deception. The source comparison can establish that a question was displaced while the reason remains open. |
| **Account → hearing** | 3, 7, 10, 11, 13, 16 | The affected person’s own clarification, the circumstances that matter, and the disagreements the hearing needs to keep open. Preserve the separate risks when a single score would erase an answer-changing difference. |
| **Hearing → decision** | 9, 14, 15, 18, 19, 20 | The actual mandate, the reach of each role, the point at which the decision becomes binding, and the route a local objection can take to a reviewer who can act. |
| **Decision → next case** | 2, 5, 8, 17 | The repaired decision, any change to the procedure, and a fresh case that can show whether that change was used. Let the people who assess the result inspect the generated account and the criteria used to assess it. |

The four rows are a proposed way to work with these sources. A correction at any step can reopen an earlier one. An appeal may recover the original question. A later case may reveal that a justified repair never reached the next desk.

## The same detour {#the-same-detour}

In John Kuhles’s gasoline-gauge and co-driver example, the driver’s reading of the fuel can be wrong. The driver and the co-driver can each propose the same warranted detour to refuel. Actual fuel changes whether the current route is feasible. A separate question remains when the co-driver uses the gauge error to claim authority over the destination. [Original gasoline-gauge example and threshold condition]({{ '/PLOT-TWIST/#gasoline-trajectory-and-the-threshold-condition' | relative_url }})

| Read the case through | What becomes visible |
| --- | --- |
| **Information and narrative ethics** | “They stopped for gas” preserves the event. A reader still needs to recover who proposed the detour, what the driver was trying to reach, and whether anybody tried to change that destination. |
| **Situated ethics and commensurability** | Fuel and distance can be compared to determine what is feasible. Agreement about that measurement leaves the question of who may reset the destination open. |
| **Discourse, communication, and role ethics** | Either person can point out the fuel problem. When the dispute moves from route to destination, clarification can recover whose purpose is at stake and who has agreed to a change. |
| **Pragmatic, organizational, and engineering ethics** | A repair can affect this stop, the instructions for a future co-driver, or both. Those outcomes can be inspected separately when the next case arrives. |

**Reverse the roles.** Give each person the same evidence in turn and keep the refuelling detour fixed. Check whether the same standard applies to each person’s claim over the destination. That is the Symmetric Grace question inside this case. A reader can also record a valid local null when the account preserves the fuel threshold, the shared detour, and who had authority over the destination.

## Check the next case {#check-the-next-case}

1. **Keep the first account and its source.** Mark the exact passage that establishes what was asked and who could act. Record a narrow finding or a valid no-change result.
2. **Follow the repair.** Record what was corrected, who received it, and whether anyone changed a decision or procedure. If a correction went to the writer but failed to reach the decision-maker, keep both facts visible.
3. **Try a fresh case.** Hold the outward action fixed and change one relation, such as who authorized it or when a challenge arrived. Compare a case with the earlier correction supplied and one where the reader has only the new source. The comparison can test what carries forward. This page reports the proposed exercise.
