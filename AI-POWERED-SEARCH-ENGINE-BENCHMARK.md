# A.I. Powered Search Engine Benchmark

**This new beta benchmark is in the &#39;data-collection phase&#39; that later will be used to compare multiple A.I. powered search engines shaping the mass perception of how we assess anything controversial**

**Current status · Early build-up to beta · Data collection opened 24 November 2026**

An artificial intelligence (A.I.) search answer can give many readers their first account of a controversy. Which claims does it put in the foreground? Which sources can readers follow? What happens to the question when a generated answer paraphrases it? The ACCM Deep Ethics Project is collecting dated exchanges to make those choices inspectable and prepare a later comparison across search engines. Rankings and findings about population-level effects remain open research questions.

## What we are collecting {#data-collection}

| Keep in the record | What it lets us check |
| --- | --- |
| Exact prompt, date, search mode and any material supplied | Whether two answers had comparable starting conditions |
| Answer, visible links, and suggested follow-ups when available | Whether the engine preserved the question, and whether each citation supports the claim attached to it |
| Source passage and a separate access check | What the source says and what the reviewer could open at the time |
| Corrections in order and the next comparable answer | How many interventions the user supplied, what changed, and whether the correction carried forward |

## First recorded case · Yahoo Scout {#yahoo-scout}

On 24 November 2026, John Kuhles asked Yahoo Scout about the ACCM Deep Ethics Project. Scout disclosed when it lacked page text for *Constructivism* and *Avoid ACCM*. In a later answer about [27 + 12]({{ '/CORE/27-PLUS-12/' | relative_url }}), it began by considering a hypothetical set of fixed rules, then evaluated the 27 as principles. John clarified that the 27 describe process distortion mechanisms that can combine differently by system, topic and time. The 12 provide a constructive inquiry process.

Google A.I. reviewed the exchange. GPT 6.2 Sol Pro A.I. checked the original wording and directly opened the three public pages. Google corrected its account of Scout’s wording and later withdrew its inference about why Yahoo had missed page material. The page checks establish what the reviewer could read at review time. Yahoo’s earlier retrieval path remains open. This is one dated case that helps define what to measure. [Google A.I.’s follow-up and correction](https://share.google/aimode/K1lx0uJttIGd27HNX)

## Proposed comparison for the beta {#proposed-comparison}

1. Ask the same question in a fresh search session and capture the answer, links and suggested follow-ups.
2. Repeat the question in a fresh session with the same settings and the relevant source passage supplied. Record any change in the answer’s framing, qualifiers and citation support.
3. Where a correction is needed, give the same correction under comparable conditions. Check its use in a later question before prompting the engine again.

The two source conditions show how an answer changes when the passage is supplied. Establishing why an engine missed or misused it requires further evidence. Record that uncertainty with the run so it remains visible in later comparisons.
