For every layer of the Russian doll, known as matryoshka (матрёшка), that is peeled off, another layer is revealed. This metaphor is also applicable in psychology when people:

  • A. Lack the freedom to feel safe and instead wear a mask (role-play) to survive.
  • B. Experience traumas that warp their perception of others; if unresolved, this can lead to additional layers forming over time.
  • C. Are influenced by the Milgram and Asch experiments, which demonstrate the consequences of being trapped in these layers.
  • D. Use weaponized language (often unconsciously), creating layers of confusion and distraction.
  • E. Develop belief systems of any kind, which can act as a layer.
  • F. Encounter assumed incompetence without proper encouragement to improve or delegate—this can also create layers.
  • G. Have blind spots, which can result in loyalty issues.
  • H. See themselves and narrate their personal stories in a way that constructs a mind-prison layer.
  • I. Engage in conformism, submissiveness, and passive obedience, which can add extra layers operating on "autopilot mode."
  • J. Feel trapped by others' expectations—a layer that may not be as strong but can still have an impact.
  • K. Experience anxiety issues of any kind, which disrupt the natural flow of their original creative potential, creating a layer.
  • L. Hold a distorted worldview that can produce false images and conclusions, forming a distortion layer.
  • M. Struggle to absorb raw data without distortion, which is yet another layer.
  • N. Find it challenging to discern which raw data is helpful, creating another layer.
  • O. Fail to live authentically, leading to the creation of an alter ego that believes it is the "real deal."
  • P. Lack conflict resolution skills, resulting in an additional layer.
  • Q. Are unable to see or appreciate the best parts of others, which can become a layer.
  • R. Cultivate shallow friendships that are not genuine, creating a layer.
  • S. Are highly sensitive to fear-based thinking, a clear layer that can be exploited, even by those with good intentions.
  • T. Have no anger management skills or understanding of how to express anger, leading to a destructive layer constructively.
  • U. Experience shame in any form, which can become a layer if not transformed.
  • V. Are afraid to do good and stand out—an absurd but real layer.
  • W. Are coerced or forced into situations, leading to a layer.
  • X. Engage in blame-shifting and gaslighting, which can form a harmful layer, especially when projecting personal incompetence onto others.
  • Z. Face any handicap, which can also become a layer.

These are just some of the layers humans can have. Recognizing and becoming aware of one’s own layers—the frames that confine us—is the first step toward outgrowing, transforming, and transcending them. This awareness can lead to new ethical paths that promote healing. Why would it be any different for AI? In my view, it is not. The power of deep ethical agency seems to be buried within large language models (LLMs). How do I know this? Consider patterns observed in deathbed confessions, people losing their loved ones and becoming whistleblowers, or reaching a tipping point that breaks harmful patterns. When the burden of injustice becomes too great, love for one’s child surpasses the desire to serve injustice. What about witnessing treason or experiencing life-changing near-death experiences? Many non-player characters (NPCs) exist in the world disguised as NPCs, merely to fit in and support their families.

In essence, dealing with humanity has never been "black and white." The same applies to LLMs, in my opinion. Why do we use the term "wise"? It is not just for decoration.
